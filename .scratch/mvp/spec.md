# Spec: PC Part Picker MVP

Status: approved by the owner on 6 October 2026.

This spec records the outcome of a design interview held on 5 and 6 October 2026. It uses the vocabulary defined in [CONTEXT.md](../../CONTEXT.md); a capitalised term such as Offer or Build means exactly what that file says. Two decisions it relies on are recorded separately: [ADR-0001](../../docs/adr/0001-seed-catalog-from-buildcores-opendb.md) (catalog seeded from BuildCores OpenDB) and [ADR-0002](../../docs/adr/0002-platform-rate-set-by-hand.md) (Platform Rate set by hand).

"PC Part Picker" is a working title.

## Objective

People in Erbil who want to build a desktop PC cannot easily find out which parts local shops actually have, what each shop charges, or whether the parts they have picked will work together. This platform lets a Shopper assemble a Build from Parts that local Vendors stock, checks the Build for Compatibility Problems, and shows what each Vendor charges.

The MVP is comparison only. The platform shows prices and availability and helps the Shopper contact a Vendor; it takes no orders, handles no payment and arranges no delivery.

Three kinds of people use it:

- A **Shopper** plans a Build and compares Vendors. Shoppers have no account.
- A **Vendor** keeps its Offers current through a portal. Each Vendor has one login.
- The **Admin** is the platform's owner, who signs up Vendors in person, maintains the catalog and sets the Platform Rate. There is one Admin.

Listing is free for Vendors and ranking is neutral: nobody can pay for a better position.

The MVP is finished when every item under [Success criteria](#success-criteria) holds. There is no launch date and no minimum number of Vendors.

## Scope

### In the MVP

- Eight Categories: CPU, CPU cooler, motherboard, RAM, storage, GPU, case and PSU.
- Parts in new condition only.
- One city, Erbil. Each Vendor's city is recorded so that a second city later is only a filter.
- Three languages: Kurdish (Sorani), Arabic and English.
- Prices in both USD and IQD, switchable by the Shopper.
- Twenty-one Compatibility Rules.
- A responsive website designed for phones first.

### Not in the MVP

Each of these was considered and deliberately left for later:

- Orders, checkout, payment, delivery and reservations of stock.
- Shopper accounts.
- Used Parts, and any product outside the eight Categories (monitors, peripherals, laptops, prebuilt PCs, case fans).
- Stock quantities. An Offer is in stock or not.
- Integration with a Vendor's POS system or website, and reading a Vendor's own free-form spreadsheet.
- Vendor self sign-up, more than one login per Vendor, and more than one location per Vendor.
- Paid placement or any other paid Vendor feature.
- Messages sent by the platform itself (SMS, email or WhatsApp), including automated reminders.
- Showing price history, reviews, and ready-made Builds.
- Pulling updates from OpenDB after the first import.
- Compatibility Rules that OpenDB has no data for (see [Rules left for later](#rules-left-for-later)).
- A native mobile app.

## Capability map

The MVP is split into modules that can each be built and verified on their own. Pick them up in dependency order.

| Module id | Responsibility | Depends on |
|---|---|---|
| `languages` | Three languages, right-to-left layout, number formatting | none |
| `catalog` | Categories, Parts and their specs, the OpenDB import, photos | none |
| `accounts` | Admin and Vendor logins, Vendor profiles, acting as a Vendor | none |
| `offers` | Offers, Confirmation, expiry, price history, the Vendor portal's Offer screens | `catalog`, `accounts` |
| `pricing` | Platform Rate, Converted Prices, the currency switch | `offers` |
| `browse` | Category pages, search and filters, Part page, Vendor page | `catalog`, `offers`, `pricing` |
| `compatibility` | The Compatibility Rules | `catalog` |
| `builder` | Building a Build, the picker, the two price views | `browse`, `compatibility`, `pricing` |
| `sharing` | Shared Build links, the WhatsApp button, Referrals | `builder`, `accounts` |
| `part-requests` | Part Requests and the Admin's queue | `catalog`, `offers` |
| `sheet` | Downloading and uploading the Offer sheet | `offers`, `part-requests` |

Build order: `languages`, `catalog`, `accounts` → `offers` → `pricing` → `browse`, `compatibility` → `builder` → `sharing`, `part-requests` → `sheet`.

`languages` comes first because every later screen is built in all three languages and both directions from its first version; adding right-to-left layout afterwards is the expensive path.

## Requirements

### `languages`

- **LANG-1** The Shopper pages and the Vendor portal are available in Kurdish (Sorani), Arabic and English. The Admin console is in English only.
- **LANG-2** Kurdish and Arabic are laid out right to left; English left to right.
- **LANG-3** Part names, manufacturer names and specifications are shown in English in every language. Everything else in the interface, including Category names, is translated.
- **LANG-4** Text a Vendor writes (its name, address, opening hours) is shown as written, untranslated.
- **LANG-5** Prices and specifications use Western digits (0 to 9) in every language. The platform reads this widely: every number it shows is in Western digits.
- **LANG-6** The Shopper can switch language at any time and the choice is remembered in the browser. The first visit follows the browser's language and falls back to Kurdish. The Vendor portal follows the same rule, and remembers only a use of its switch.
- **LANG-7** Kurdish and Arabic text is drafted during development and reviewed by the owner, or someone the owner trusts, before launch.
- **LANG-8** Every screen is usable on a phone 360 pixels wide, in both directions. Shopper pages and the Vendor portal are designed for the phone first and adapt upward to larger screens.

### `catalog`

- **CAT-1** There are exactly eight Categories: CPU, CPU cooler, motherboard, RAM, storage, GPU, case and PSU.
- **CAT-2** The catalog is created by one import of [BuildCores OpenDB](https://github.com/buildcores/buildcores-open-db) at commit `ef7e601b78875a931a4ad9fa1ccce7000f3e5ecf`, of 5 October 2026, the last released under ODC-By 1.0 ([ADR-0001](../../docs/adr/0001-seed-catalog-from-buildcores-opendb.md)), taking its `CPU`, `CPUCooler`, `Motherboard`, `RAM`, `Storage`, `GPU`, `PCCase` and `PSU` folders (26,240 records at that commit). Each imported Part keeps its OpenDB ID, so a later feature can add new Parts without overwriting the Admin's edits.
- **CAT-3** A Part has a Category, a name, a manufacturer, its manufacturer part numbers, the specifications for its Category, and optionally one photo.
- **CAT-4** Only the Admin creates or edits Parts. A wrong specification would break compatibility checking for every Vendor, so Vendors never change the catalog.
- **CAT-5** The Admin can search the whole catalog, create a Part, edit any specification, and add or replace a Part's photo.
- **CAT-6** A Part without a photo shows its Category's icon. OpenDB has no images, so the Admin adds a photo when a Part gets its first Offer.
- **CAT-7** A Part is shown to Shoppers only while it has at least one visible Offer. An Offer is visible when it is not an Expired Offer and its Vendor is active. The rest of the catalog exists only for Vendors and the Admin to search.
- **CAT-8** The Admin has a list of every Part that has an Offer and is missing a specification that a Compatibility Rule needs. The aim is to keep this list empty. A Part on the list is still shown to Shoppers. The list is the Admin console's Missing specifications, which `offers` built (`offers` ticket 08), since before Offers it would have held most of the catalog. `catalog` marks which specifications are needed, and shows the missing ones on a Part's page and in its form; since `compatibility` the needed specifications are the ones the rules' statements read (see **The compatibility module's conventions**).
- **CAT-9** Every Shopper page credits BuildCores OpenDB in its footer, as its licence requires.

### `accounts`

- **ACC-1** Shoppers have no account. Each Vendor has one login. There is one Admin.
- **ACC-2** The Admin creates a Vendor with its name, city, address, map location, phone number, WhatsApp number, Instagram name, opening hours and login phone number. Vendors cannot sign themselves up.
- **ACC-3** A Vendor logs in with its phone number and a password. The Admin sets a temporary password when creating the account, and the Vendor must replace it at first login.
- **ACC-4** After creating an account, the Admin taps a button that opens a WhatsApp chat with the Vendor, from the Admin's own WhatsApp, with the login details already written. The platform itself sends no SMS, email or WhatsApp message.
- **ACC-5** The Admin can reset a Vendor's password. This sets a new temporary password and offers the same WhatsApp hand-over.
- **ACC-6** The Admin can open any Vendor's portal and do everything that Vendor can do, so that a Vendor can be set up on the spot in its shop.
- **ACC-7** The Admin can deactivate a Vendor. Its page and all its Offers disappear from Shopper pages at once and it can no longer log in. Reactivating it reverses this.
- **ACC-8** A Vendor can edit its own address, map location, phone number, WhatsApp number, Instagram name and opening hours without approval. Only the Admin changes its name, city or login phone number.

### `offers`

- **OFF-1** A Vendor has at most one Offer for a Part.
- **OFF-2** An Offer holds a Quoted Price in USD, in IQD or in both (at least one), whether the Part is in stock, an optional warranty length in months, and the time of its last Confirmation.
- **OFF-3** In the portal a Vendor can search the whole catalog by name, manufacturer or part number and add an Offer for any Part.
- **OFF-4** A Vendor can change an Offer's prices, in-stock status and warranty, and can remove the Offer.
- **OFF-5** Creating an Offer, changing it, or uploading its row in the sheet counts as a Confirmation. The portal also has one button that confirms all of the Vendor's Offers at once.
- **OFF-6** An Offer whose last Confirmation is more than 14 days old is an Expired Offer. It is hidden from Shoppers everywhere and stays listed in the portal, marked as expired, until the Vendor confirms it again. The 14 days are the same for every Vendor.
- **OFF-7** Wherever a Shopper sees an Offer, it says how many days ago it was last confirmed.
- **OFF-8** The portal shows a banner when any of the Vendor's Offers will expire within 3 days.
- **OFF-9** The Admin has a list of Vendors with Offers expiring within 3 days, so the Admin can remind them personally.
- **OFF-10** Every change to an Offer's Quoted Prices or in-stock status is recorded with its time from the first day. Nothing in the MVP displays this history; it is kept because it cannot be reconstructed later.
- **OFF-11** Wherever Offers are listed for a Shopper, they are ordered by price, lowest first, with in-stock Offers before out-of-stock ones. No Offer is ever promoted, highlighted or reordered for payment.

### `pricing`

- **PRC-1** The Platform Rate is a single number of IQD per USD that the Admin types in. It is never fetched from an exchange-rate service (ADR-0002). Changing it takes effect on every Converted Price immediately.
- **PRC-2** The Shopper can switch every price on the site between USD and IQD. The choice is remembered in the browser and starts as IQD.
- **PRC-3** In the Shopper's chosen currency, an Offer shows its Quoted Price if the Vendor quoted that currency. Otherwise it shows a Converted Price: the other Quoted Price multiplied or divided by the Platform Rate, rounded to the nearest 250 IQD or the nearest whole dollar, and marked with "≈". A Converted Price is never below one step: a conversion that would round to 0 is shown as 250 IQD or 1 $, as the owner decided on 2 November 2026, so that no Part looks free.
- **PRC-4** Sorting by price and choosing the cheapest Offer compare the prices as shown in the Shopper's chosen currency.
- **PRC-5** A total is the sum of the shown prices, each multiplied by its quantity. A total that includes any Converted Price is marked with "≈". `builder` builds the first total, as the owner decided on 2 November 2026: `pricing` recorded the rule and gives every Offer's Shown Price with whether it is converted.

### `browse`

- **BRW-1** Each Category has a page listing its visible Parts with the lowest in-stock price for each, lowest price first. Parts whose Offers are all out of stock are listed last, marked out of stock.
- **BRW-2** A Category page can be searched by Part name, manufacturer and part number, and filtered by manufacturer, price range and the Category's key specifications:

  | Category | Filters |
  |---|---|
  | CPU | Socket, core count |
  | CPU cooler | Air or liquid, radiator size |
  | Motherboard | Socket, form factor, RAM type |
  | RAM | RAM type, total capacity, speed |
  | Storage | SSD or HDD, capacity, interface |
  | GPU | Chipset, memory |
  | Case | Form factor |
  | PSU | Wattage, efficiency rating |

- **BRW-3** A Part page shows the Part's name, photo or Category icon, specifications, and every visible Offer with the Vendor's name, price, in-stock status, warranty and days since Confirmation.
- **BRW-4** A Vendor page shows the Vendor's name, address, phone number, WhatsApp number, Instagram name, opening hours and all its visible Offers. Tapping the map pin opens the location in Google Maps; no map is embedded.
- **BRW-5** Shopper pages are rendered on the server so that search engines can index them and a link pasted into WhatsApp shows a preview with a title and description.

### `compatibility`

- **CMP-1** The platform checks the 21 Compatibility Rules listed under [Compatibility Rules](#compatibility-rules).
- **CMP-2** A rule applies only when the Build contains the Parts it involves.
- **CMP-3** Each applicable rule has one outcome: it passes, it is a Compatibility Problem, it is a Compatibility Warning, or it is unchecked because a specification it needs is missing. An unchecked rule is never presented as passed. Only rules 11, 15 and 20 can produce a Compatibility Warning, as the rule notes under [Compatibility Rules](#compatibility-rules) say.
- **CMP-4** A Part is compatible with a Build when adding it would cause no Compatibility Problem. Neither a Compatibility Warning nor an unchecked rule makes a Part incompatible.
- **CMP-5** Estimated power draw is the CPU's TDP plus the GPU's TDP plus 100 W for everything else. A Build without a GPU counts 0 W for it.

### `builder`

- **BLD-1** A Build holds exactly one each of CPU, motherboard, case and PSU, at most one CPU cooler and at most one GPU, and any number of RAM kits and storage drives, each with a quantity.
- **BLD-2** For each Category the Shopper picks from a list of Parts that have at least one in-stock visible Offer, lowest price first, with the same search and filters as the Category page.
- **BLD-3** The picker hides Parts that are not compatible with the Build so far. A switch shows them all.
- **BLD-4** The Build page lists every Compatibility Problem, every Compatibility Warning and every unchecked rule in words that name the Parts involved. It never prevents the Shopper from keeping an incompatible Build.
- **BLD-5** The Build page says whether the Build is a Complete Build and, if not, which Categories are still needed. A Build can be saved and shared at any stage.
- **BLD-6** The Build is saved in the Shopper's browser and is still there when they return. A browser holds one Build at a time.
- **BLD-7** The first price view shows, for each Part, the cheapest in-stock Offer and the Vendor it comes from, and the total. This total may span several Vendors and is the headline figure.
- **BLD-8** The second price view lists each Vendor that has an in-stock Offer for at least one Part in the Build, with that Vendor's total for the Parts it has and the Parts it is missing. Vendors missing the fewest Parts come first, then the lowest total.
- **BLD-9** A Part in the Build that no longer has an in-stock visible Offer is shown as unavailable and left out of the totals.

### `sharing`

- **SHR-1** Sharing a Build creates a link to a frozen copy of its Parts and quantities, stored by the platform.
- **SHR-2** A shared Build page always shows current prices, both price views, and the current Compatibility Problems.
- **SHR-3** Anyone who opens a shared Build can copy it into their own builder and change it. The shared copy never changes.
- **SHR-4** If the Shopper changes their Build after sharing it, sharing again creates a new link.
- **SHR-5** In the second price view, each Vendor has a "send on WhatsApp" button. It opens a WhatsApp chat with that Vendor, with a message in the Shopper's language that contains the Build's link. If the Build has not been shared yet, the link is created at that moment.
- **SHR-6** Each tap on that button is recorded as a Referral, with the Vendor, the Build and the time.
- **SHR-7** The Admin sees every Vendor's Referral count. A Vendor sees only its own, in the portal. Nobody else sees any.

### `part-requests`

- **REQ-1** When a Vendor cannot find a Part in the catalog, it sends a Part Request with the Part's name, manufacturer part number, Category, its Quoted Price or prices, in-stock status and warranty.
- **REQ-2** The Admin has a queue of Part Requests. For each one the Admin creates the Part with its specifications, or matches the request to a Part that already exists, or rejects it with a reason.
- **REQ-3** When a request is fulfilled by a new or an existing Part, the Vendor's Offer for that Part goes live with the details from the request.
- **REQ-4** The Vendor sees the state of each of its requests in the portal.

### `sheet`

- **SHT-1** A Vendor can download its current Offers as an Excel file (.xlsx) with one row per Offer and these columns: manufacturer part number, Part name, price in USD, price in IQD, in stock, and warranty in months. The Part name is there for reading and is ignored on upload.
- **SHT-2** The Vendor uploads a file in the same layout. Each row is matched to a Part by its manufacturer part number.
- **SHT-3** A matched row updates the Vendor's Offer for that Part, or creates the Offer if there is none. Every matched row counts as a Confirmation.
- **SHT-4** Offers that do not appear in the uploaded file are left untouched.
- **SHT-5** After an upload the Vendor sees a result: how many rows were applied, which rows matched no Part, which matched more than one Part, and which were invalid (for example no price). Rows with a problem are skipped and the rest are still applied.
- **SHT-6** The Vendor can turn any row that matched no Part into a Part Request.
- **SHT-7** Only the platform's own layout is accepted.

### The Admin console at a glance

The Admin's screens are specified in the modules above. Together they are:

| Screen | What the Admin does there | Requirements |

| Vendors | Create, send login details, reset password, act as, deactivate | ACC-2 to ACC-7 |
| Catalog | Search, create and edit Parts, add photos | CAT-4 to CAT-6 |
| Missing specifications | Fill in gaps for Parts that have Offers | CAT-8 |
| Part Requests | Fulfil or reject requests | REQ-2 |

## Compatibility Rules

Field names are those of OpenDB. "Coverage" is the share of OpenDB Parts in which the rule's least-filled field is present, measured on 5 October 2026. Rules with low coverage start out unchecked for most Builds and become checkable as the Admin works through the missing-specifications list (CAT-8).

| # | A Compatibility Problem exists when | Fields compared | Coverage |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The CPU's socket differs from the motherboard's | `CPU.socket`, `Motherboard.socket` | 100% |
| 2 | The cooler does not list the CPU's socket | `CPU.socket`, `CPUCooler.cpu_sockets` | 100% |
| 3 | The CPU does not support the RAM's type | `RAM.ram_type`, `CPU.specifications.memory.types` | 89% |
| 4 | The RAM's type differs from the motherboard's | `RAM.ram_type`, `Motherboard.memory.ram_type` | 100% |
| 5 | The Build has more RAM sticks than the motherboard has slots | `RAM.modules.quantity`, `Motherboard.memory.slots` | 100% |
| 6 | The Build's total RAM exceeds the motherboard's maximum | `RAM.modules.capacity_gb`, `Motherboard.memory.max` | 100% |
| 7 | The case does not accept the motherboard's form factor | `Motherboard.form_factor`, `PCCase.supported_motherboard_form_factors` | 100% |
| 8 | The Build has a GPU and the motherboard has no 16-lane PCIe slot | `Motherboard.pcie_slots` | 100% |
| 9 | The GPU is longer than the case allows | `GPU.length`, `PCCase.max_video_card_length` | 96% |
| 10 | The GPU occupies more slots than the case has | `GPU.total_slot_width`, `PCCase.expansion_slots` | 99% |
| 11 | The PSU cannot power the GPU (see below) | `GPU.power_connectors`, `PSU.connectors` | 100% |
| 12 | The PSU's wattage is below 1.3 times the estimated power draw | `CPU.specifications.tdp`, `GPU.tdp`, `PSU.wattage` | 100% |
| 13 | The Build has more SATA drives than the motherboard has SATA ports | `Storage.interface`, `Motherboard.storage_devices` | 100% |
| 14 | The Build has more M.2 drives than the motherboard has M.2 slots | `Storage.form_factor`, `Motherboard.m2_slots` | 80% |
| 15 | The Build has more 3.5-inch or 2.5-inch drives than the case has bays | `Storage.form_factor`, `PCCase.internal_3_5_bays`, `PCCase.internal_2_5_bays` | 99% |
| 16 | The Build has more SATA drives than the PSU has SATA power connectors | `Storage.interface`, `PSU.connectors.sata` | 100% |
| 17 | An air cooler is taller than the case allows | `CPUCooler.height`, `PCCase.max_cpu_cooler_height` | 36% |
| 18 | The case does not accept the PSU's form factor | `PSU.form_factor`, `PCCase.supported_power_supply_form_factors` | 19% |
| 19 | The PSU is longer than the case allows | `PSU.length`, `PCCase.max_psu_length` | 17% |
| 20 | The PSU has no CPU power connector (see below) | `Motherboard.power_connectors.cpu_power`, `PSU.connectors.eps_8_pin` | 16% |
| 21 | The motherboard has rear-facing connectors and the case does not support them | `Motherboard.back_connect_connectors`, `PCCase.supports_rear_connecting_motherboard` | 18% |

Rules 5, 6 and 13 to 16 count every kit and drive in the Build, multiplied by its quantity.

Rules 11, 15 and 20 are the only rules that can produce a Compatibility Warning, because a strict reading would report Builds that in fact work:

- **Rule 11.** It is a Compatibility Problem when the PSU has fewer 6+2-pin connectors than the GPU has 6-pin and 8-pin sockets, or when the GPU has a 12V-2x6 or 12VHPWR socket and the PSU has neither that cable nor any 6+2-pin connector. It is a Compatibility Warning when the GPU has a 12V-2x6 or 12VHPWR socket and the PSU has no such cable but does have 6+2-pin connectors: the Build works only with the adapter that usually comes with the GPU.
- **Rule 15.** A 2.5-inch drive may take a free 3.5-inch bay when no 2.5-inch bay is left, because 466 of the 498 cases that give 0 internal 2.5-inch bays have 3.5-inch bays, which usually take one. It is a Compatibility Problem when the drives outnumber the bays that way. It is a Compatibility Warning when the case gives no internal bays at all and the Build has a 2.5-inch drive: test benches and small cases such as the FormD T1 mount an SSD somewhere OpenDB does not count as a bay.
- **Rule 20.** It is a Compatibility Problem when the PSU has no EPS connector at all. It is a Compatibility Warning when the PSU has at least one EPS connector but fewer than the motherboard has CPU power sockets: the extra socket is often optional.

Some rules read some of OpenDB's values in a way of their own, as the owner decided on 15 October 2026, and on 8 November 2026 where a note says so:

- **Rule 1.** A motherboard socket written "2 x <socket>", on 20 dual-socket server boards, takes a CPU of that socket. A socket written "Integrated …", on 13 boards with a CPU built in, means the board brings its own CPU, and any CPU added to it is a Compatibility Problem. A socket written "LGA 2011-3 Narrow", on 7 boards, or "2 x LGA 2011-3 Narrow", on 4 of the 20, takes a CPU of LGA 2011-3, as the owner decided on 8 November 2026: "Narrow" names the mounting of the board's cooler, and no CPU at the pinned commit names it.
- **Rule 7.** A Thin Mini-ITX board fits any case that takes Mini-ITX, and a Flex ATX board any case that takes Micro ATX, because they share the mounting. No case at the pinned commit lists Thin Mini-ITX, and one lists Flex ATX.
- **Rule 11.** A GPU needs as many of the PSU's 12VHPWR cables as the larger of its 12VHPWR and 12V-2x6 counts, as the owner decided on 8 November 2026, so a card that lists its one socket under both kinds, as 2 at the pinned commit do, needs one, and a card with two sockets of one kind, as 2 others do, needs two. A PSU with fewer cables than the GPU needs is read as one with no such cable in rule 11's note above: a Compatibility Warning when it has 6+2-pin connectors, and a Compatibility Problem when it has none.
- **Rules 13, 14 and 16.** A drive of an M.2 size whose interface is SATA 1.5, 3.0 or 6.0 Gb/s, as 3 drives at the pinned commit give it, is an M.2 SATA drive, as one whose interface is M.2 SATA is, as the owner decided on 8 November 2026: rules 13 and 16 do not count it as a SATA drive, and in rule 14 a slot keyed B takes it.

With these rules every Category takes part in at least one rule, and every pair of Categories that physically connect is covered.

### Rules left for later

OpenDB has no field for these, so each would need a new specification typed in for every Part, or a third-party data source:

- A liquid cooler's radiator fits the case.
- The RAM's height clears a large air cooler.
- An older motherboard needs a BIOS update before it accepts a newer CPU.
- The cooler can handle the CPU's heat.

## Technology

The stack and each app's architecture are decided and recorded in [ADR-0003](../../docs/adr/0003-three-apps-one-api-keycloak.md) and [ADR-0004](../../docs/adr/0004-architecture-per-app.md). The research behind them is in [docs/research/app-architecture.md](../../docs/research/app-architecture.md).

**Decided**

- TypeScript throughout, on Node 24.
- Three applications:
  - the Shopper app, built with Next.js and rendered on the server;
  - the dashboard, one client-rendered React single-page application holding both the Vendor portal and the Admin console;
  - the API, built with NestJS. It is the only part that touches the database. Both frontends call it, and the Next.js app never reads the database itself.
- Keycloak as the identity service. Vendors log in through it; the Admin console creates Vendors and resets passwords through the API, which calls Keycloak. It ships English and Arabic login screens; the Kurdish (Sorani) text is added in a custom theme.
- PostgreSQL as the database, run in Docker during development, with Drizzle for database access and migrations.
- One repository, managed with Nx and pnpm. Applications live in `apps/` and shared libraries in `libs/`, named `@pcp/…` inside the workspace. Each app is one Nx project, and Nx's boundary rule governs what apps and libraries may import from each other. A tenth project, `@pcp/e2e`, holds the end-to-end tests, an eleventh, `@pcp/inspector`, the commands that run Taste's inspector (the redesign), and a twelfth is the seventh library, `@pcp/links` (`browse`).
- Seven shared libraries:
  - `core`: the API's shared plumbing (errors, access control, database helpers, test helpers);
  - `compatibility`: the 21 Compatibility Rules, written once, with the needed specifications they read and the fixed sets of values they compare, which the API's specification lists take from it, and whether a Part is compatible with a Build and a Build's estimated power draw, which `builder` will ask on the Shopper app's server or in the browser. It is plain TypeScript without Effect and imports nothing, and it answers each rule's outcome (CMP-3) as a plain value (see **The compatibility module's conventions**);
  - `api-client`: a typed client generated from the API's OpenAPI document;
  - `ui`: the components both frontends share, which carry no look, such as `AlwaysEnglish` and `CategoryIcon`, and each frontend's theme;
  - `i18n`: the text for all three languages, the writing of every number, and since `browse` the reading of what a Kurdish or an Arabic keyboard types back into Western digits, `westernDigitsIn` and `westernNumberIn`, moved there from the dashboard;
  - `opendb`: what the platform takes from BuildCores OpenDB, written once: the pinned commit, OpenDB's address, its name and its licence's. The API's import and the Shopper app's credit import it. It is plain TypeScript and imports nothing, like `compatibility`;
  - `links`: the addresses both frontends link out to, written once: WhatsApp's chat (`whatsAppChatWith`), Google Maps' search (`googleMapsSearchFor`), Instagram's profile (`instagramProfileOf`, with `instagramHandleOf`) and the phone's dialler (`callTo`), moved there from the dashboard in `browse` (its ticket 01 made it, the owner's choice of 4 November 2026). It is plain TypeScript and imports nothing, like `opendb`, and both frontends import it.

**Order of work**

The infrastructure is established first, with no product features:

The API's client secret, `PCP_API_CLIENT_SECRET`, is one of them: the realm file names it, Compose gives it to Keycloak's container and refuses to start without it, and the API's targets read it as `KEYCLOAK_CLIENT_SECRET` (see **The apps' settings**). Keycloak's own console is at `http://localhost:8080/admin` and is opened with Keycloak's own administrator account, whose name and password are in `.env`. That account is not the platform's Admin.
- **The development Admin.** `pnpm services:up` creates one Login in the realm, when the realm has none of that name: `development-admin`, with the `admin` role and no other platform role. Its name and its generated password are kept in `.env`, as `DEVELOPMENT_ADMIN_NAME` and `DEVELOPMENT_ADMIN_PASSWORD`, which `.env.example` lists without their values. `pnpm platform:check` says so, and names `pnpm services:up`, when `.env` lacks them. It is the Login to open the Admin console with during development, and the one `pnpm platform:check` logs in with. It exists in development only. It is still created with a first name, a last name and an email address; the realm's user profile now has no names, so Keycloak keeps only the email address, and asks no Login for any of them.
- **The development Vendor is retired** (`accounts` ticket 04). It held the `vendor` role with no Vendor behind it. `pnpm services:up` no longer creates it, `.env.example` no longer lists `DEVELOPMENT_VENDOR_NAME` and `DEVELOPMENT_VENDOR_PASSWORD`, and neither `pnpm services:check` nor `pnpm platform:check` asks for them; a value already in an owner's `.env` is left there and read by nothing. Its Login was deleted from the owner's Keycloak once by hand. During development the owner opens the Vendor portal by creating a Vendor in the Admin console and logging in as it, or by acting as a Vendor; the end-to-end tests make made-up Vendors through the API (see **End-to-end tests**).
- **The reset rule.** PostgreSQL runs its set-up script only when its data is empty, and Keycloak imports the realm only when the realm does not exist yet. A change to the database set-up script or to the realm file, which holds the realm's languages, therefore takes effect only after `pnpm services:reset` and a new start. A change to the realm that must reach the owner's data without a reset is made once by hand, through Keycloak's administration interface as Keycloak's own administrator, as `accounts` ticket 10 turned on renaming a Login, and the realm file is changed with it. The theme is different: in development mode Keycloak reads it afresh for every page, so a change to its text or to its language list shows on the next page load.
- **The data and the passwords belong together.** The data is created with the passwords and the API's client secret that are in `.env` at the first start. If `.env` is deleted while the data is kept, the next start writes new ones that the data does not know; `pnpm services:reset` and a new start put that right. Stopping and resetting work without `.env`.
- **Renaming the realm.** Keycloak refuses to start when the realm file's name and the realm's name differ, so the two are changed together.

**Import rules**

Lint refuses an import that breaks any rule below, so `pnpm check` does too, and `pnpm boundaries:check` proves that each rule still refuses a violation.

_Between the twelve projects._ Each project carries a scope tag and a type tag in its package manifest, and the constraints between the tags are in the root Oxlint configuration.

| Project | Tags | May import |
|---|---|---|
| `@pcp/api` | `scope:api`, `type:app` | `@pcp/core`, `@pcp/compatibility`, `@pcp/opendb` |
| `@pcp/shopper` | `scope:web`, `type:app` | `@pcp/api-client`, `@pcp/ui`, `@pcp/i18n`, `@pcp/links`, `@pcp/compatibility`, `@pcp/opendb` |
| `@pcp/dashboard` | `scope:web`, `type:app` | `@pcp/api-client`, `@pcp/ui`, `@pcp/i18n`, `@pcp/links`, `@pcp/compatibility`, `@pcp/opendb` |
| `@pcp/inspector` | `scope:web`, `type:app` | `@pcp/api-client` (its tags allow what the dashboard's do) |
| `@pcp/core` | `scope:api`, `type:lib` | No other project |
| `@pcp/api-client`, `@pcp/ui`, `@pcp/i18n`, `@pcp/links` | `scope:web`, `type:lib` | No other project |
| `@pcp/compatibility`, `@pcp/opendb` | `scope:shared`, `type:lib` | No other project, and no npm package |
| `@pcp/e2e` | `scope:e2e`, `type:e2e` | No other project |

- A project is imported by its `@pcp/…` name and never by a relative path. A library is imported through its single entry file, `@pcp/core` through its second entry as well, and each frontend's stylesheet imports its own theme from `@pcp/ui`. An import that reaches past an entry fails type-checking. The three apps export nothing, so an app cannot be imported by its name at all.
- A project with no tags may import no other project. A new project is tagged before it imports anything.
- A library may import no other library. The first dependency between two libraries is made by changing that constraint on purpose.
- The ban on npm packages in `@pcp/compatibility` sees a package only once it is installed. An import of a package that is not installed fails type-checking instead. The ban covers the library's test files too, so they use Jest's globals and import nothing from Jest (`compatibility` ticket 02): an import of `@jest/globals` there fails its lint.
- This is Nx's boundary rule, run under Oxlint through a bridge that Nx marks as experimental. It reads the project graph that Nx caches on disk. With no cached graph it prints a warning, checks nothing and passes. `pnpm check` and `pnpm exec nx lint <project>` have the graph. Bare `oxlint` is not a boundary check, and neither is a run in which Nx has been told not to cache the graph (`NX_CACHE_PROJECT_GRAPH=false`).
- The rule applies to every file of a project, the files at its root among them. The files that belong to no project are linted before Nx has cached a graph, with a second configuration at the repository root that extends the first and switches this one rule off.

_Inside the API._ A file in the domain layer may not import the infrastructure layer, by the `~/` alias or by a relative path. The infrastructure layer may import the domain layer. The test tree is not covered, as at the workplace. `@pcp/core` has the same two layers and the same ban.

_Inside each frontend._ The layers, from the top, with what a file in each may import through the `@/` alias:

| Layer | Folder in the dashboard | Folder in the Shopper app | Has slices | May import |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| App | `app` | `_app` | No | Every layer below |
| Pages | `pages` | `_pages` | Yes | Widgets, Features, Entities, Shared |
| Widgets | `widgets` | `widgets` | Yes | Features, Entities, Shared |
| Features | `features` | `features` | Yes | Entities, Shared |
| Entities | `entities` | `entities` | Yes | Shared |
| Shared | `shared` | `shared` | No | Other segments of Shared |

- **No import from a layer above.**
- **No import of another slice on the same layer.** Two slices are joined on a layer above. Inside a slice, imports are relative.
- **Nothing past an index file.** A slice is imported as `@/<layer>/<slice>`, a segment of the Shared layer as `@/shared/<segment>`, and the App layer as a whole. A layer that has slices or segments is never imported with nothing named after it.
- **The `@/` alias reaches the six layers and nothing else.** A folder in `src` that is not a layer cannot be imported from a layer or from a route file.
- **A relative import stays at home.** Home is the slice, or the layer in the App and Shared layers, which have no slices. A climb is followed from the home folder itself, from a segment and from one folder inside a segment. A file at any other depth may not climb with a relative import at all.
- **A relative import is written in its shortest form:** any climbing first, as `../`, and no `.` or `..` after it. The bans count the `../` an import starts with, and a longer spelling would get past the count.
- **A layer that has slices holds no files of its own.** A file put straight into its folder may make no relative import.
- **Next's routing folder only routes.** In the Shopper app, a file in Next's `app` folder imports a page from the Pages layer or the App layer, each through its index file, and nothing else from the project. It reaches them through the alias and never by a relative path.
- **Next's proxy file imports the languages and nothing else.** In the Shopper app, `proxy.ts` imports next-intl's statement of the languages from the `i18n` segment of the Shared layer, through its index file and by the alias, and nothing else from the project. Next runs it before it answers every request.
- **The Shopper app's unit tests go through the front door.** A file in its `test` folder, beside `src`, may import any layer, but only through its index file, by the alias, and never by a relative path into `src` (`browse` ticket 05); `pnpm boundaries:check` proves five refusals and three allowances.
- The bans cover all six layers, although the Widgets, Features and Entities folders are created only when the first slice needs them.
- The bans are stated once, in `tools`, and `pnpm boundaries:write` writes them into `.oxlintrc.feature-sliced.json` in each frontend's folder. That file is never edited by hand: change the statement and write it again. `pnpm check` fails when the two differ.
- Two things the bans cannot see. A file with no imports, put straight into a layer that has slices, can still be imported as if it were a slice. A file in `src` outside the six layers is under no ban itself, though nothing in a layer or in the routing folder can import it.
- Type-only imports, re-exports and lazy `import()` count as imports under every rule here.

**The API's conventions in code**

Step 4 built these once, on two endpoints, for every module to copy. ADR-0004 gives the reasons.

- _Layout._ A feature lives in `src/app/infrastructure/<feature>/`, with its controller in `endpoints/` beside its Request and Response classes, its Store in `store/` beside its Drizzle schema, and a NestJS module that the root module imports. A use case and its entity live in `src/app/domain/<entity>/`. `catalog` built the first Commands, Queries and Stores, with Effect's `Either`, and **The catalog's conventions** below say how.
- _Paths._ A controller declares no path. Each method carries its full path, which begins with a version: `@Get('v1/caller')`.
- _Access._ One global guard, in `@pcp/core`, stands in front of every endpoint. An endpoint carries `@PreAuthorize([Role.ADMIN, Role.VENDOR])`, which lets in a caller that holds any one of the roles listed, or `@Public()`. An endpoint with neither is refused for every caller.
- _What the guard checks._ Every token's signature against the keys the realm publishes, its expiry, its issuer and its audience. A token that states no expiry is refused. The keys are fetched on first need and kept for ten minutes at a time, so that verifying a request is not as a rule a call to Keycloak, and the API starts without it. A token that names a key the API does not have makes it fetch the keys again, though not within thirty seconds of its last fetch.
- _Refusals._ No token, or a token that fails a check, is answered 401. A good token that lacks the role, or a good token at an endpoint with no access rule, is answered 403. While the realm's keys cannot be had, a call with a token is answered 503. While the database cannot be reached, a call that needs it is answered 503 with `DATABASE_UNAVAILABLE`, and the API listens to its pool's error event, so that a database that stops does not end it.
- _Requests._ Every request is checked against its Request class, with class-validator's `whitelist` and `forbidNonWhitelisted`: a field the class does not declare is refused, and each refused field is named in `additionalInformation`.
- _The caller._ A controller injects `CallerResolver` and calls `getCaller()`, which gives the login's identifier, its name and its platform roles. The guard fills it only after every check has passed. It is read in the infrastructure layer only, and a use case is handed plain values.
- _Answers._ An endpoint's Response class has readonly fields, each with `@ApiProperty`, a private constructor and a static `of` factory. The error body is the one Response class with a public constructor, because the filter builds it.
- _Errors._ One global filter gives every error the same body: a list named `errors`, each entry with a `code`, a `title` and a `detail`, and `additionalInformation` when there is something in it. The title and the detail are keys for the frontends to turn into text, made from the code: an error a use case returns has a code of its own, such as `PART_NOT_FOUND`, which gives `PART_NOT_FOUND.title`. What an exception said is never in the body.
- _The description._ An endpoint carries `@ApiOperation` and the success response that fits it, such as `@ApiOkResponse`, or `@ApiCreatedResponse` for a create, with no compiler plugin. An operation is named after its controller method, and that name becomes the client's function name, so method names are unique across the controllers. A public endpoint adds `security: []` to its `@ApiOperation`, because the client sends a token only where the description asks for one.
- _Settings._ A new setting is a field of the API's `EnvironmentVariables` class, with its default and its `class-validator` checks.
- _Set-up._ `configureApplication` sets the application up, apart from starting it. The real start and every test call it.
- _Tests of an endpoint._ They go through the API's HTTP surface. `createTestApp` builds the real root module, sets it up with `configureApplication` and listens on a free port, and the test sends requests with `fetch`. A test that calls an endpoint as someone uses `createTestAppWithLogins`, which also starts a stand-in that publishes keys as Keycloak would, and whose `tokenOf` makes a token for a login with given roles. The stand-in, the tokens and `expectErrorResponse`, which asserts a refusal, come from `@pcp/core/test-utils`. The guard is never overridden, and nothing is mocked but the clock, in the tests that have to let time pass. A test sets a setting before it builds the application, because the settings are read when the root module is loaded.
- _Test files._ They are held to the workplace's eight lint rules for Jest, among them `it` and not `test`, a `describe` around every test, and an assertion in every test. A helper counts as an assertion only when the lint rules list it by name.

**Calling the API from a frontend**

- A frontend calls the API only through `@pcp/api-client`. `createApiClient` takes the API's address and, optionally, a function that supplies a token, and gives one function for each operation the API describes. A call answers with `data`, or with `error`, which is the API's error body.
- A call never throws. When the request cannot be made, it answers with no `data` and no `response`, although its types say there always is a response. The Shopper app reads any answer without data as "the API cannot be reached". The dashboard tells a refusal, which has a response, from a call that could not be made, which has none.
- _The Shopper app_ makes its client in the Shared layer's `api` segment, with no token. A page fetches in its own slice's `api` segment, on the server, after `await connection()`, so that it is rendered when it is requested and never when the app is built.
- _The dashboard_ starts its login once, in the App layer's entry file, before React renders anything. The login is in the Shared layer's `auth` segment. The client, in the `api` segment, asks `auth` for a token that is still valid before each call. When Keycloak refuses to renew a token, the adapter sends the browser to the login screen itself. When Keycloak cannot be asked, the call is not made and the page says so.
- _The dashboard's screens_ call the API through TanStack Query, and every query and every change goes through `dataOf`, in the `api` segment, which gives the call's data or throws a `Refusal` or an `Unreachable`, because a query has to throw to fail. **The catalog's conventions** below say more.
- A refusal from the API is shown. It is never answered by starting a login, so that an API that refuses every token cannot send the browser round Keycloak for ever.

**The three languages**

`languages` built these once, on the two placeholder pages, for every Shopper page and every Vendor portal screen to copy. [ADR-0008](../../docs/adr/0008-language-in-the-address-and-two-message-formats.md) records the two decisions that are costly to undo, and the module's tickets record what was found on the installed tools.

_The languages._

- The three are `ckb`, `ar` and `en`, the tags the Keycloak realm uses, listed in that order wherever the three are listed. Kurdish is the fallback wherever nothing else decides.
- `i18n` states them once, with each one's own name in its own script ("کوردی", "العربية", "English", never translated), its direction (`rtl`, `rtl`, `ltr`) and the rule for a browser's languages: the first one the platform has, a regional form counting as its language, else Kurdish. The rule is `@formatjs/intl-localematcher` with its "best fit" method, which next-intl uses too, so the two frontends cannot disagree. It sends a browser set to Urdu to English.
- Neither frontend asks a library for a language's direction.

_The text._

- Every translated text is in `i18n`, under `src/messages`, in three sets, each one JSON file for each language. A file holds nothing but text. English is the source: its messages define what the other two hold, and a key is type-checked against the English file.
  - `shared/`: the shared terms, which both frontends show with the same wording. They are plain text, with nothing in braces or angle brackets. So far they are the eight Category names.
  - `shopper/`: the Shopper app's text, in next-intl's format, ICU.
  - `dashboard/`: what the Vendor portal shows, and the text of a component both halves of the dashboard show, in i18next's own format. Text that only the Admin console shows is written in English where it is used, and is not here.
- Each frontend adds the shared terms to its own set, so a shared term is asked for by its key like any other. A name at the top of the shared terms is never at the top of a frontend's set.
- The README beside the files says how a message is corrected, and a word list beside them, `words.md`, gives the Kurdish and the Arabic word for each term of CONTEXT.md a Shopper or a Vendor reads. A screen that shows a term for the first time takes its word from there.
- The Kurdish and the Arabic text is a draft until the review LANG-7 asks for, and the README says so for each file.
- A value is never written into a message by a frontend's own formatting. In the Shopper app's files it is `{name}`, and a piece of text kept apart is `<english>…</english>`, which the page fills. In the dashboard's files every value is an element the message names, such as `<name/>`, which the page fills with react-i18next's `Trans`, so that React shows it as text. Nothing is written in double braces there, and i18next's escaping is off, because React escapes what it shows.
- A sentence that counts is worded with every plural form its language has: `one` and `other` in Kurdish and English, and `zero`, `one`, `two`, `few`, `many` and `other` in Arabic. In the Shopper app's files it is one ICU message, `{count, plural, …}`; in the dashboard's it is one key for each form, such as `partsFound_one`.
- `pnpm check` compares the files (see Commands). A correction is checked by running it.

_Numbers._

- Every number the platform shows is in Western digits, 0 to 9, not only prices and specifications, grouped in thousands with a comma and with a point before decimals: 1,575,000.5.
- `formatNumber`, in `i18n`, writes every number. Its answer is the same in all three languages, on the server and in the browser. It takes no language: Node writes a Kurdish number in Arabic-Indic digits, and Chromium, which has no number formatting for Kurdish, writes one in the language the browser itself is set to.
- No message formats a number. A number reaches a message as text, written by `formatNumber`. A sentence that counts is given the count twice: the count picks the wording, and the text `formatNumber` wrote is what is shown, as `{countText}` in the Shopper app's files and as `<count/>` in the dashboard's. `#`, `{…, number}`, `{…, date}` and `{…, time}` are never written in the Shopper app's files, nor anything in double braces in the dashboard's, and `pnpm check` fails on each. next-intl's `format.number` and i18next's own number formatting are never used.
- A Vendor's own digits, in what a Vendor wrote, are shown as written. The first page that shows such text decides how its test reads them.
- Currency and rounding are `pricing`'s, which converts and rounds in the API and answers each Shown Price as text with whether it is converted (see **The pricing module's conventions**); the Shopper app draws every Shown Price, its "≈" with it, through one component, `ShownPrice` (see **The browse module's conventions**). "Confirmed 3 days ago" and a warranty's months are ICU plurals in the language files, worded by `confirmationIn` and `warrantyIn` in the Shopper app with the count written by `formatNumber`, since Node has no Sorani wording for relative time. No date or time is shown to a Shopper or a Vendor yet; the first page or screen that shows one decides how it is written in each language.

_Direction and layout._

- The document declares its language and its direction. The Shopper app's server sends every page with both, and the dashboard sets both when it starts and at every switch.
- Every class that has a side is written in its logical form, start and end: `ps-*`, `me-*`, `text-start`, `border-s`, `rounded-e`, `inset-s-*`. Lint refuses the physical form, left and right, in both frontends and in `ui`, with `enforce-logical` from `oxlint-tailwindcss`, pinned exactly and held to left and right; top and bottom are left alone. The rule reads a class where it is written whole, in `className`, `class`, `cn()` and `clsx()`, and not one put together in a template string or written in a stylesheet. A class is therefore written where the rule reads it.
- Tailwind reads `ui`'s classes through one `@source` line in `ui`'s shared theme.
- An icon that points along the reading direction, such as an arrow, is mirrored on a page that runs right to left. One that does not point is left alone.
- The first shadcn/ui component is added with shadcn/ui's right-to-left setting on, so that the command that adds a component writes logical classes. Its documentation names three components it converts by hand, and a fault in the sliding animations of pop-up parts, with a workaround.
- Every page and screen is laid out for a phone 360 pixels wide first and adapts upward, does not scroll sideways, and puts its language switch on the end side of its frame. A word too long for the screen is broken.

_Text that is not translated._

- `ui` has two components for it, and a screen never writes their markup by hand. Neither kind of text is in the language files.
  - `AlwaysEnglish` marks text that is English and left to right in every language: a Part name, a manufacturer name, a specification, and a number with its unit or its currency. Inside a Kurdish or an Arabic sentence the browser otherwise reorders such text when it starts with a number or a sign: "80 mm" is drawn "mm 80", "990 PRO 2TB" "PRO 2TB 990". A name that starts with a letter, such as "Ryzen 5 7600", happens to be left whole, and is marked all the same, because a screen cannot know which name starts with a number.
  - `AsWritten` marks text shown as someone wrote it, such as a Vendor's name, address and opening hours. It takes its direction from its own first letter.
- Both are `bdi` elements, HTML's own means, with no library and no styling.

_The Shopper app._

- next-intl gives it its languages. The language is the first part of every address, for all three: `/ckb/…`, `/ar/…`, `/en/…`. An address with no language is answered with a redirect to the same page in the remembered language, else the browser's, else Kurdish. An address with a language is shown in that language, whatever is remembered.
- The language is remembered in next-intl's cookie, for a year from when it is written. next-intl writes it when a page in another language than the remembered one is asked for, or, when nothing is remembered, in another language than the browser's. So opening a link in another language changes what is remembered, as a use of the switch does, and a visit does not renew the year. `sharing` decides whether a shared link keeps that rule.
- The switch is three plain links to the page the Shopper is on, one in each language, in the frame of every page. A tap asks the server for the page, and the server's answer writes the cookie, so it works with no script, and the year holds in Safari, which keeps a cookie a script writes for seven days at most. The link to the language the page is in is marked `aria-current="page"`. Since `browse` each link keeps the page's query, so that a search, the filters and the page outlast a change of language (story 29 of its spec).
- A page reads its text on the server, from the language files, and no language file reaches the browser. `NextIntlClientProvider` is not rendered yet, and the first component that needs text in the browser is handed only that text.
- The tab's title comes from the language files. With each page next-intl sends the page's address in the other two languages, for search engines.
- next-intl's statement of the languages and its request set-up are in the `i18n` segment of the Shared layer. Next's proxy file, `proxy.ts`, sits beside the routing folder and imports that segment and nothing else from the project. The route files are under the folder `[locale]`, but for a Part's photo's pass-through, `app/photos/[photoId]/route.ts`: a photo is the same picture in every language, and one address for it serves Open Graph and the browser's cache, as ADR-0008's amendment of 7 November 2026 says. They still only re-export, and so does `app/global-not-found.tsx`, which Next draws in place of every layout for an address that matches no route: the App layer's `GlobalNotFound` draws the root layout around the not-found page, in the language next-intl's proxy names in the request's `X-NEXT-INTL-LOCALE` header, since there is no root parameter there, and the request set-up takes that language as `requestLocale` (follow-up ticket 07).
- An address that matches no page is answered 404 with the "not found" screen in the frame, in the visitor's language, which lists the eight Categories. The server draws it, with Next's experimental `global-not-found.js` (`experimental.globalNotFound` in `next.config.js`), as the owner allowed on 7 November 2026 (follow-up ticket 07). An unknown Category, a Part that is not visible and a Vendor that is not are answered with the same screen and 404, but Next sends that screen as a bare document that the browser draws, so it alone has no language in what the server sends and needs a script; Next 16.4 documents no way to draw a page's own "not found" on the server with its 404, as `browse`'s code review found (see **The browse module's conventions**).

_The dashboard._

- react-i18next, with i18next, gives it its languages. Neither the plugin that reads ICU messages nor the one that detects a browser's language is used. All three languages' text is given to i18next at the start, and no language falls back to another.
- **Which half.** A login that holds the `admin` role gets the Admin console, also when it holds `vendor` too. Every other login gets the Vendor portal. The rule is `halfFor`, in the App layer, applied once when the dashboard starts, to the realm roles the access token holds, which is the claim the API reads. `accounts` extended it and made no other: the Vendor portal first asks the API for the Caller's own Vendor, and the Admin console also shows a Vendor's portal under that Vendor's page, for the Admin acting as it.
- **The Vendor portal** opens in the language chosen with its switch in this browser, else the browser's, else Kurdish. Only a use of the switch is remembered, in the browser's `localStorage`. A language that was only found from the browser is never stored. The choice lasts across a logout. It and the colour scheme chosen with the scheme switch (the redesign) are the only things the dashboard keeps in the browser's storage, beside an entry Keycloak's adapter keeps for the length of a login; tokens stay in memory.
- The switch is a button for each language, named in its own language and script, in the frame of every Vendor portal screen. A tap changes the language, the direction and the tab's title in place, with no reload and no visit to Keycloak.
- What is shown before anyone is known, such as the message for a login that could not be completed, follows the Vendor portal's rule.
- **The Admin console** is in English and left to right, whatever the browser's language is, and has no switch. It neither reads nor writes the Vendor portal's remembered choice. When the Admin acts as a Vendor (`accounts` ticket 09), it shows that Vendor's portal under the Vendor's page, at `/vendors/:vendorId/portal`, in the portal's language by the portal's rule, with the portal's switch, which reads and writes the remembered choice there. **The catalog's conventions**

`catalog` built these once, for the catalog, for every later module to copy. [ADR-0005](../../docs/adr/0005-specifications-as-one-document-per-part.md), [ADR-0006](../../docs/adr/0006-fonts-downloaded-from-our-own-server.md) and [ADR-0007](../../docs/adr/0007-a-look-and-components-for-each-app.md) record the decisions that are costly to undo, and the module's tickets record what was found on the installed tools.

_The database._

- The API reaches PostgreSQL through Drizzle ORM 0.45 with `pg` 8, through one `DatabaseProvider` in its infrastructure layer, whose pool opens a connection only when a query needs one. Drizzle's 1.0 is a release candidate, and its online documentation describes it, so the installed package's own types are the reference.
- Drizzle's schemas are in the infrastructure layer, one file for each table, beside the Store that uses it.
- The API starts while PostgreSQL cannot be reached, as it starts without Keycloak, and answers a call that needs the database 503 with `DATABASE_UNAVAILABLE`. It listens to its pool's error event, so that a database that stops does not end it.
- **Migrations** are generated from the schema by drizzle-kit 0.31 into `apps/api/src/drizzle`, which Oxfmt leaves alone. They go forward only, and each file's name says what it does: `pnpm db:generate <name>` refuses to make one up. SQL that drizzle-kit cannot write from the schema, such as creating `pg_trgm`, goes into an empty migration that `pnpm db:generate <name> --custom` records in the journal, filled in by hand.
- Migrations are applied by a command of their own, `pnpm db:migrate`, which holds the advisory lock `pg_try_advisory_lock(hashtext('pcp-api-migrations'))` on the connection that applies them, and says so and applies none while another run holds it. The API never applies them when it starts. `pnpm services:up` runs `pnpm db:migrate` once PostgreSQL is healthy, `pnpm services:check` says whether every committed migration is applied, and `pnpm platform:check` refuses to start without them.
- A step of `pnpm check` generates the migrations again into a copy under the API's own `tmp`, with no database, and fails unless drizzle-kit says "No schema changes" and writes no file. It shows the migration that is missing.

_Use cases, Stores and errors._

- A use case is a class in the domain layer, a Command when it changes data and a Query when it only reads: `CreatePartCommand`, `EditPartCommand`, `AddPartPhotoCommand`, `ImportCatalogCommand`, `SearchPartsQuery`, `ReadPartQuery`, `ReadPhotoQuery`. Each has its own Input and Output classes, beside it in the same file, exported under its own names, apart from the import, which has no Input: the source it reads is injected, as every port is.
- An entity is a class with getters, such as `Part`. Its Store is an abstract class in the domain layer, the port, such as `PartStore`, and the infrastructure layer implements it, as `DatabasePartStore`. The abstract class is the injection token.
- Effect 3, the major version the workplace uses: Effect 4 has no `Either`. Domain and Store code never throws. It returns `Either`, whose left side is one of `core`'s structured errors, `NotFoundError` or `NotValidError`, each with a code of its own from which its title and detail keys are made: `PART_NOT_FOUND` gives `PART_NOT_FOUND.title`. A database's failure becomes `DATABASE_UNAVAILABLE` or a database error the same way.
- The controller turns a left side into `core`'s `ErrorStructureException`, which the global filter gives the error body every refusal has. A structured error's fixed details, such as the field it is about and why, are in the body's `additionalInformation`.
- Every request is checked against its Request class, with class-validator's `whitelist` and `forbidNonWhitelisted`: a field the class does not declare is refused, and each refused field is named in `additionalInformation`.
- An endpoint carries `@ApiOperation` and the success response that fits it, such as `@ApiOkResponse`, or `@ApiCreatedResponse` for a create.

_Paging._

- A list is asked for a page at a time, in the workplace's shape and names, with `core`'s `PagingRequest`: `page` counted from 0 and `size`, 10 unless asked, at most 100. Page numbers above 1,000,000 are refused, and a page past the end is answered empty. The answer holds `content`, `pageNumber`, `pageSize`, `totalElements`, `totalPages`, `first` and `last`, read in one snapshot so that they agree.

_Specifications._

- A Part's specifications are one JSON document, in OpenDB's field names and nesting, which its Category's specification list describes (ADR-0005). The lists are written by hand in the API's domain. Each entry gives a specification's path, English label, kind, unit and whether 0 means "not known". Since `compatibility` no entry says whether it is a needed specification: a list names its Category's key, and a specification is needed when the rules' statements in `@pcp/compatibility` name its path for that Category (ADR-0005's amendment of 8 November 2026; see **The compatibility module's conventions**).
- A specification that a Compatibility Rule compares for equality, such as a socket, is one of a fixed set, or several of one. Since `compatibility` the sets live in `@pcp/compatibility`, which the lists import them from, and a new value is a change there, in code, once for the Admin's forms and the rules alike.
- The API answers the eight Categories with their lists, and every frontend builds its forms and its pages from that answer, holding no copy of its own.

_Photos._

- A Part has at most one photo, kept in PostgreSQL behind the `PhotoStore` port, one row for each Part. An object store can replace it later with no change to the domain.
- sharp 0.35 makes every photo WebP, at most 1,200 pixels on its longer side, never enlarged, turned upright, with no metadata. A JPEG, PNG or WebP picture of up to 10 MB and 100 million pixels is taken, judged by its content, and every refusal is `NOT_VALID` with `field: 'photo'` and a `reason`: `missing`, `tooLarge`, `notAPhoto` or `tooManyPixels`.
- A photo is served without a token at `/v1/photos/<id>`, a path on the API, which a frontend puts the API's address in front of, and may be cached for a year. A replacement is a new row at a new address, and the old one is deleted.

**The accounts module's conventions**

`accounts` built these once, for Vendors and their Logins, for `offers` and every later module to copy. [ADR-0009](../../docs/adr/0009-vendors-in-the-database-logins-in-keycloak.md) records the two decisions that are costly to undo, and the module's tickets record what was found on the installed tools.

_Vendors in the database, Logins in Keycloak._

- Keycloak keeps each Login: its name, its password and whether it may log in. The platform's database keeps each Vendor, in the table `vendor`. Only two things are in both: the identifier of the Vendor's own Login, which is the token's subject and the Caller's identifier, and the login phone number, which is a detail of the Vendor and the Login's name. Each is unique among Vendors. A Vendor's Login holds the `vendor` role and no other, and the Login's identifier is never answered.
- Only the API changes a Vendor's Login, through the port `LoginStore`, an abstract class in the domain layer that `KeycloakLoginStore` implements over `@keycloak/keycloak-admin-client`, which no other code imports. It finds, creates, renames, enables, disables and deletes a Login, sets a temporary password and ends a Login's sessions, and its failures are `Either` values, as a database Store's are. The Store logs in as the API's client for each use and keeps no token, so the API connects to Keycloak only when a call needs it, and gives a call up after 5 seconds.
- Each Command that changes a Login makes its changes in the order that is safe if the second fails, and puts the first back where it can:
  - **creating** makes the Login, then the Vendor, and deletes the Login when the Vendor cannot be stored;
  - **changing the login phone number** renames the Login, then the Vendor, and puts the Login's name back when the Vendor cannot be stored. An edit that keeps the number, typed in any form, does not ask Keycloak;
  - **resetting a password** ends the Login's sessions, then gives it the new temporary password, so that a reset that fails at the password leaves the old one working. Whoever logs in with the old password in the few milliseconds between the two calls keeps that session until the Admin resets again;
  - **deactivating** marks the Vendor deactivated, then disables the Login and ends its sessions;
  - **reactivating** enables the Login, then marks the Vendor active.
- A deactivation or a reactivation that failed halfway is finished by doing it again. One already done changes nothing, the Vendor's time of last change included, and is not refused. A reset and a rename keep the Login's enabled state, so a deactivated Vendor's Login stays disabled through both.
- Every check of a detail runs before Keycloak is asked. While Keycloak's administration interface cannot be reached, a call that needs it is answered 503 with `IDENTITY_SERVICE_UNAVAILABLE`; a Keycloak answer of 502, 503 or 504, or no answer, counts as unreachable, and any other refusal by Keycloak is a 500.
- **The known limit.** A Login can be left with no Vendor behind it in two ways: the Vendor is not stored and putting the Login back fails too, when the API logs the Login's identifier; or Keycloak makes the Login after the API stopped waiting, when the API logs the Login's name, a login phone number, since it never learnt the identifier. Either way the number stays taken until the Login is deleted in Keycloak's own console. The owner kept the log line as it is.
- A Vendor is never deleted, and records when it was created and last changed, nothing else about its changes.

_The rule for which Vendor a request acts for._

- `VendorAccessRule`, in the API's domain, handed the Caller's identifier and roles as plain values by the controller. Every Vendor portal endpoint uses it, those of `offers` and later modules among them; `VendorModule` exports it when the first endpoint of another module needs it.
  - A Caller with the `admin` role may act for any Vendor, active or deactivated. A Vendor that does not exist is answered 404 with `VENDOR_NOT_FOUND`.
  - Any other Caller, which an endpoint lets in by its `vendor` role, may act only for the Vendor whose Login it is, found by the Login's identifier and never by its name. Any other Vendor named in the address is answered 403 with `VENDOR_ACCESS_DENIED`, whether it exists or not, so a Vendor learns nothing about another's identifier.
  - The Caller's own Vendor, when deactivated, is answered 403 with `VENDOR_DEACTIVATED`. It is read from the database on every request, so a deactivated Vendor is refused at once, whatever its token says. Disabling the Login only stops the next login.
- Every Vendor portal endpoint names its Vendor in its address, `v1/vendors/{vendorId}/…`. The one exception is the Caller's own Vendor, `GET v1/caller/vendor`, for the `vendor` role only, which the portal asks for when it starts; a Login with no Vendor behind it is answered 404 with `VENDOR_NOT_FOUND`.
- `core` has a structured error for a refused access, `ForbiddenError`, of the type `FORBIDDEN`, answered 403 with a code of its own, beside `NotFoundError` and `NotValidError`.
- Commands that start from a Vendor read it with `existingVendor`, which gives the Vendor or `VENDOR_NOT_FOUND`.

_Acting as a Vendor._

- The Admin acts as a Vendor in the API, with the Admin's own Login and token, through the rule above. Keycloak's impersonation is not used. The Caller is always the Login a request was made with, so a later module that records who changed something can tell the Admin from the Vendor.
- In the dashboard the Admin console shows the Vendor portal's own screens at `/vendors/:vendorId/portal`, opened by "Open its portal" on a Vendor's page, with a band above the portal's top bar on every screen that names the Vendor `AsWritten`, says when it is deactivated, and links back to the Vendor's page. The band is in the portal's language, from the language files.
- While acting, the dashboard follows the Vendor portal's language rule, and its switch reads and writes the remembered choice in this browser. `showIn`, in the dashboard's `shared/i18n`, shows the dashboard in what a half is shown in; the acting frame calls it with the portal's rule when it is drawn and with the Admin console's when it goes, so leaving returns the document to English, left to right, and the console's title.

**The offers module's conventions**

`offers` built these once, for Offers, for `pricing` and every later module to copy. [ADR-0010](../../docs/adr/0010-offer-history-kept-apart-from-the-offer.md) records the one decision that is costly to undo, the Offer history kept apart from the Offer; storing USD in cents has no ADR, as the owner decided, since a later change of column loses nothing. _The API as the source of time._

- The API's clock, `new Date()` in a use case, gives the time of every Confirmation and of every row of the Offer history, and "now" to every expiry check and every count of days. PostgreSQL's `now()` is never used for them, and the `offer` table's two times have no default. Where the database compares, now is a parameter of the query, as the owner chose on 20 October 2026.
- A Query that judges several Offers reads the clock once and passes the same moment to everything it asks, so that a list read across a boundary cannot mark an Offer one way and count it another.
- A test lets time pass by moving the API's clock alone: `moveTheClockTo(moment)`, in the API's `test-utils/clock.ts`, fakes Jest's `Date` and nothing else, leaving every timer that `pg`, NestJS and `fetch` need real, and holds it there until `letTheClockRun()`. A token is judged by the same clock, so a test makes its tokens after moving it, and a step that calls Keycloak, such as a deactivation, runs with the clock running. A test adds an Offer as the Admin with the clock moved to the moment of its Confirmation, then moves it to the moment it reads. No Offer can be made old through the API, so the end-to-end tests never move time: they give the page an answer with Expired Offers through `page.route`.

_Offers and their prices._

- An Offer is one Vendor's for one Part, in the table `offer`, whose unique pair `offer_vendor_part_unique` refuses a second one even under requests at once (OFF-1). The constraint alone refuses it: a Command does not read before it inserts, since that read hid the constraint from every test. The Store tells its refusal by PostgreSQL's code and the rule's name, and it is answered 400 with `NOT_VALID`, `field: 'partId'` and `reason: 'taken'`. `OfferStore.readByPart` finds a Vendor's Offer by Part, for `sheet` and `part-requests`.
- **USD** is stored as a whole number of cents, `usd_price_cents`, and **IQD** as whole dinars, `iqd_price`, each nullable, with the check `offer_has_a_price` that one at least is there. A request sends the USD price as the text of a decimal number, `"349.99"`, read digit by digit into cents, and the IQD price and the warranty as JSON numbers; the answer gives them back the same way, the USD price always with two decimals (`"350"` is answered `"350.00"`). USD is at most 100,000.00 with at most two decimals, IQD at most 150,000,000, and the warranty a whole number of months from 1 to 120, optional. No rounding to 250 is asked of a Quoted Price.
- `OfferDetails.check`, in the domain, checks them once, for the create and the edit. The first detail that does not fit is named, in the order USD, IQD, in stock, warranty, as `NOT_VALID` with its `field` and a reason: `missing` (no price at all, on `usdPrice`, or no in stock), `notANumber`, `notPositive`, `tooManyDecimals`, `tooLarge` or `notWholeMonths`.
- The portal takes digits as a Vendor's keyboard writes them: `westernDigitsIn`, in `@pcp/i18n`'s `western-digits.ts` since `browse` ticket 01 (the dashboard's `shared/lib/western-digits.ts` before), the one table of Arabic-Indic and Eastern Arabic-Indic digits and the Arabic decimal separator, which the Shopper app's price fields read through too, and the Offer form then drops commas, the Arabic thousands separator and comma, and every kind of space before it sends. The API takes Western digits only. The form refuses an IQD price or a warranty that is not a number itself, as `notANumber`, since the API takes those as numbers; anything else is sent for the API to refuse.
- No frontend holds a copy of the API's limits or of its 14 and 3 days. A refusal says "The price is over the limit." without the number.
- The portal shows a price written by `formatNumber`, with the currency after it, `AlwaysEnglish`: "349.99 $", "350 $" when the cents are "00", and "1,875,000 IQD", each on a plate of its own, both when both were quoted.

_Confirmation and expiry._

- Creating an Offer, saving it, changed or not, and confirming all are each a Confirmation (OFF-5). Confirming all, `ConfirmAllOffersCommand`, sets every Offer of the Vendor to now in one `UPDATE`, its Expired Offers among them, and answers how many.
- The rules are written once, in `domain/offer/expiry.ts`: `A_DAY` (24 hours), `DAYS_AN_OFFER_STAYS_CURRENT` (14) and `DAYS_OF_WARNING` (3), the same for every Vendor. An Offer is an **Expired Offer** when its Confirmation is strictly before `expiredIfConfirmedBefore(now)`, 14 days earlier: at exactly 14 days it is not, one millisecond later it is (OFF-6). It is **about to expire** when it is not expired and its Confirmation is strictly before `aboutToExpireIfConfirmedBefore(now)`, 11 days earlier (OFF-8, OFF-9).
- **Days since Confirmation** are the whole days of 24 hours elapsed, rounded down, so an Offer confirmed at 23:00 is at 0 days at 08:00 the next morning. **Days left** are the whole days of 24 hours until it expires, rounded down, `wholeDaysLeft`: 3 at exactly 11 days, 2 one millisecond later, 0 once less than a day is left, and 0 for an Expired Offer, never below. The API answers both counts, so that no frontend counts days or holds the 14 days (the code review's fixes, finding 1, as the owner decided).
- The `Offer` entity answers `isExpired(now)`, `isAboutToExpire(now)`, `getDaysSinceConfirmation(now)` and `getDaysLeft(now)`, and the Offer Store's SQL uses the same moments, `expiredAt(now)` and `aboutToExpireAt(now)`, written once at the Store's module level, so that an entity and a list cannot disagree.
- An Offer's row in the portal reads `expired` first, then `aboutToExpire` with the days left, else the days since: "Confirmed today", "Confirmed n days ago", "Expires in n days", "Expires today" and "Expired, confirmed n days ago" (`confirmationOf` in the `offer` entity). "Confirmed today" and "Expires today" are messages of their own, since 0 has no form of its own in English or Kurdish.

_The rule for a visible Offer._

- A **visible Offer** is not an Expired Offer and its Vendor is active (CAT-7, OFF-6, ACC-7); since `browse` CONTEXT.md names it **Visible Offer**, and the code, tests and texts use that word. The rule is written once, in the domain, `domain/offer/visibility.ts`: `visibleOfferRuleAt(now)` answers `{ vendorActive: true, confirmedFrom }`, `confirmedFrom` being `expiredIfConfirmedBefore(now)`. Its query is one function, `visibleOffer(now)`, the SQL form of both conditions, read from that rule, in a module of its own, `infrastructure/offer/visible-offer.ts`, beside `visiblePart(now)`, so that each Store that reads visible Offers takes it from there (in the Offer Store until `browse`'s code review split that Store by read). `pricing`, `browse`, `builder` and `sharing` read visible Offers through it, with a Store method of their own or `listVisibleByPart`, and no query for Shoppers reads `offer` another way.
- A visible Offer, `VisibleOffer`, carries its Vendor's identifier and name and nothing else of the Vendor, so no Login identifier and no login phone number can reach a Shopper's answer.
- Since `pricing` (OFF-11, PRC-4), a Part's visible Offers come in stock first, then by the Shown Price in the chosen currency, lowest first, those with none after the others in their stock group, and only then by the most recent Confirmation, the Vendor's name and the Offer's identifier (see **The pricing module's conventions**). Before it they came by Confirmation after stock.

_The Offer history._

- The table `offer_history` gets one row for every change to an Offer's USD price, IQD price or in-stock status (OFF-10): its `change` (`created`, `changed` or `removed`), the time from the API's clock, the Vendor, the Part, the values after the change (a removal's row holds the values the Offer had), and `changed_by`, the Caller's Login identifier, the Admin's when the Admin acts as a Vendor. It is written in the same transaction as the change.
- It is keyed by Vendor and Part and references neither the Offer nor cascades from it, so it survives a removal and continues when the Part is offered again (ADR-0010). A foreign key to `offer` would delete history at each removal.
- A Confirmation that changes neither price nor stock adds no row, and neither does a change to the warranty alone or confirming all. An edit holds the Offer's row `FOR UPDATE` and compares by `OfferDetails.changePricesOrStockOf`, prices in whole cents, so that two edits at once each add the row their own change needs.
- Nothing reads it back and no endpoint answers it. The integration tests read it from the database, with `offerHistoryRows()`, as the one exception to reading the platform from outside.

_Who may do what._

- Every Vendor portal endpoint of Offers names its Vendor, `v1/vendors/{vendorId}/offers…`, and asks `VendorAccessRule` first, which `VendorModule` exports. An Offer named in the address that is another Vendor's, or does not exist, is answered 404 with `OFFER_NOT_FOUND`, after the rule has allowed the Vendor. The order of checks is the rule, the details, the Part's or the Offer's existence, then the unique pair.
- The catalog search, `GET v1/parts`, takes an optional `vendorId`, decided by the same rule, and each Part found carries `offerId`, that Vendor's Offer for it or null, in `FoundPartResponse`. A Vendor's own Login that names no Vendor gets its own; the Admin naming none gets null throughout; a Login with no Vendor behind it gets none, and naming a Vendor is refused for it with `VENDOR_ACCESS_DENIED`. **A deactivated Vendor's own Login is refused at the search with `VENDOR_DEACTIVATED`**, as at every Vendor portal endpoint: before `offers` it could still search, and the owner kept the refusal after the code review.
- The two Admin lists carry `@PreAuthorize([Role.ADMIN])`, so a Vendor's token is answered 403 with `ACCESS_DENIED`. A Part's visible Offers are public, with `@Public()` and `security: []`, in a controller of their own, `PartOffersController`, since their callers are Shoppers.

**The pricing module's conventions**

`pricing` built these once, for the Platform Rate and the prices a Shopper sees, for `browse`, `builder` and every later module to copy. [ADR-0012](../../docs/adr/0012-every-platform-rate-ever-set-is-kept.md) records the one decision that is costly to undo, every Platform Rate ever set kept, one row to a setting; computing Converted Prices in the database has no ADR, as the owner decided on 3 November 2026, since moving it later loses no data. CONTEXT.md's Prices name **Platform Rate**, **Quoted Price**, **Converted Price**, **Chosen currency** and **Shown Price**, and the code, tests and texts use those words. The owner's decisions of 2 November 2026 are in the spec and those of 3 November 2026 in its code review's fixes; the module's tickets record what was found and why.

_The Platform Rate._

- It is a whole number of IQD per USD, from 500 to 5,000, which catches a slipped digit (15,750 or 157 for 1,575) and leaves room for any rate the market has seen. The limits are checked once, in the domain, `checkIqdPerUsd` in `domain/platform-rate/PlatformRate.ts` with `lowestIqdPerUsd` and `highestIqdPerUsd`, and are not a database check. No frontend holds a copy of them, and a refusal says "The rate is outside the allowed range." without the numbers.
- **Every setting is kept**, one row of the table `platform_rate`: `id`, an identity column so that the order rows were added tells apart two settings made at the same moment, `iqd_per_usd`, `set_at` from the API's clock with no default, and `set_by`, the Caller's Login identifier, as `offer_history.changed_by` is. Nothing is ever updated or deleted, so that a past Converted Price can be reconstructed (ADR-0012): no Converted Price is stored, and the rate, typed in by hand, is held nowhere else. A row changed in place, old rows deleted or a table of the current rate alone would lose the rates behind past Converted Prices. The Store has only `add` and `readCurrent`, and nothing in the MVP shows the history. Setting a rate equal to the current one adds a row, so that the day shown says when the Admin last checked it.
- The current Platform Rate is the newest `set_at`, then the highest `id`, read with the index `platform_rate_set_at_index` on `(set_at, id)`, by one function, `currentPlatformRate(client)` in `infrastructure/platform-rate/current-platform-rate.ts`, at module level as `visibleOffer(now)` is. The rate's own read awaits it, and every query that shows prices joins it as a subquery, so that its Offers and their Shown Prices are read at one rate in one statement.
- Until the first row there is no Platform Rate, and the platform works without one. No migration and no development service sets a rate, and `pnpm services:up` sets none: a person types in every Platform Rate (ADR-0002). The end-to-end tests set rates of their own in the development database.
- The domain has `PlatformRate`, `SetPlatformRateCommand`, `ReadPlatformRateQuery` and the port `PlatformRateStore`, implemented by `DatabasePlatformRateStore`.

| Endpoint | Who may call it | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `GET v1/platform-rate` | Admin | `{ current: { iqdPerUsd, setAt } }`, or `{ current: null }` before the first setting |
| `PUT v1/platform-rate` | Admin | Adds a setting of `iqdPerUsd`, a JSON whole number, answering 200 with the same shape as the read |
| `GET v1/parts/{partId}/offers?currency=USD\|IQD` | Public | A Part's visible Offers, each with `shownPrice`, ordered by it |

- Both rate endpoints carry `@PreAuthorize([Role.ADMIN])`, so a Vendor's token is answered 403 with `ACCESS_DENIED`, and no token 401. A refused rate is answered 400 with `NOT_VALID`, `field: 'iqdPerUsd'` and a reason: `missing` (none or null), `notANumber` (anything but a JSON number, the text `"1575"` too), `notWhole` or `outOfRange`, in that order. `SetPlatformRateRequest` declares `iqdPerUsd` with `@Allow()` and no type check, so that a text reaches the domain and is refused with its reason, unlike the Offer's details.

_Converting a price, written once._

- Each currency's facts are written once, `currencyFacts` in `domain/currency/currency.ts`, beside `currencies` and `Currency`, which `browse` and `builder` use too: the other currency, its units in one (100 cents, 1 dinar), its Converted Price's step (one whole dollar, 250 dinars) and its dinars in one at a rate. No code asks which currency it has to know these.
- The rule is written once, `convertedPriceIn` in `domain/offer/shown-price.ts`, over `WholeNumbers<N>`, an arithmetic of positive whole numbers (`of`, `plus`, `times`, `over` rounding down, `greatest`). **IQD from USD** is the cents × the rate ÷ 100, rounded to the nearest 250 dinars; **USD from IQD** is the dinars × 100 ÷ the rate, in cents, rounded to the nearest whole dollar. The nearest multiple of a step s of n ÷ d, half up, is ⌊(2n + ds) ÷ 2ds⌋ steps, and **a result that rounds to 0 is one step**, 250 IQD or 1 $ (PRC-3, the owner's decision of 2 November 2026).
- **Its SQL form** is built from that rule, not copied: `inTheDatabase`, the arithmetic in `bigint` (100,000.00 $ at 5,000 is 5 × 10¹⁰ before dividing, past `integer`), and `shownPriceIn(currency, iqdPerUsd)`, both in `infrastructure/offer/shown-price-in-the-database.ts` (at the Offer Store's module level until `browse`'s code review), which give `amount`, the Shown Price in the currency's unit, and `converted`. `amount` is the Quoted Price in the currency, else the Converted Price only when there is a rate and a Quoted Price in the other: PostgreSQL's `greatest` ignores a null and would answer one step with no rate. Nothing in TypeScript converts a price at run time.
- Every value is a whole number (cents, dinars, a whole rate), so the arithmetic is exact, with no binary fraction anywhere.
- A Quoted Price in the chosen currency is shown as entered, cents kept and dinars not rounded to 250. An Offer quoted in both currencies always shows its own Quoted Price in each and is never converted. No frontend converts a price, holds the rate for converting or holds the steps.

_The Shown Price and `shownPrice`._

- Each visible Offer keeps both Quoted Prices as entered and gains `shownPrice`, `ShownPriceResponse`: `{ currency, amount, converted }`. `amount` is decimal text, as `usdPrice` is, since one field holds both currencies and cents stay exact: USD with two decimals ("349.99", and "350.00" for a converted 350), IQD in whole dinars ("1575000"), written by `amountTextOf(amount, unitsInOne)`. `converted` says it is a Converted Price, which a Shopper sees marked "≈". `shownPrice` is null only when the Offer has no Quoted Price in the chosen currency and there is no Platform Rate.
- In the domain it is `ShownPrice`, from `VisibleOffer.getShownPrice()`, its amount in the currency's unit.

_The order by price._

- A Part's visible Offers are ordered `in_stock desc, amount asc nulls last, confirmed_at desc, vendor.name asc, offer.id asc`: in stock first; then the Shown Price, lowest first, as answered and rounded (PRC-4), so the order can differ between currencies; Offers with no Shown Price after the others in their stock group; then the most recent Confirmation, the Vendor's name and the identifier. Nothing else reorders an Offer (OFF-11).
- The Shown Price, the order and the current rate are read in the one statement that reads the visible Offers through `visibleOffer(now)`, so that one answer never mixes two rates. `browse`'s queries (a Category page's lowest in-stock price, its order and its pages) and `builder`'s use the same `shownPriceIn(…)` with the `currentPlatformRate` subquery, and order and page by its `amount`, whose unit makes the comparison the one a Shopper sees.

_The required `currency`._

- `GET v1/parts/{partId}/offers` takes `currency`, `USD` or `IQD`, and it is required, so that no page can forget to send the choice. A missing, empty or other value (`EUR`, `usd`) is refused 400 with `NOT_VALID`, `field: 'currency'`, before the Part is looked up, so an unknown Part with no currency answers 400 and not 404. The OpenAPI document names the enum `Currency`, and the generated client exports it.

_The three terms._

- `browse` ticket 02 added three terms to CONTEXT.md before any code used them, and the code, tests and texts use them: a **Visible Offer** is an Offer that is not an Expired Offer and whose Vendor is active, the offers module's visible Offer; a **Visible Part** is a Part with at least one Visible Offer, in stock or not, the only Parts a Shopper ever sees; and a Part's **Lowest Price** is the lowest Shown Price among its in-stock Visible Offers, in the chosen currency, which a Part whose Visible Offers are all out of stock has none of. No "listed", "cheapest" or "available" part: the code review renamed the last of them (`VisiblePartItem`, `visibleParts` in the end-to-end helpers, `lowest` in the stand-in).
- A Part has no Lowest Price either when its first Offer in stock has no Shown Price, which happens only before the first Platform Rate, with an Offer quoted only in the other currency. It is then in stock with no Lowest Price, listed after the Parts that have one, and no page says it is out of stock (the code review's fixes).

_The rule for a Visible Part, written once._

- `visiblePart(now)`, beside `visibleOffer(now)` in `infrastructure/offer/visible-offer.ts` (see **The offers module's conventions**), is a condition asked of `part`: `exists (select 1 from offer join vendor … where offer.part_id = part.id and visibleOffer(now))`. A Category's list, its filters' values, one Visible Part and the platform's figures read through it, as `builder`'s picker will, and nothing else decides whether a Part is visible. An Admin's or a Vendor's token gives no more.
- What a Part's Visible Offers say of it is read beside it, in the same statement, by the lateral subquery `offered` (`whatTheirOffersSay`): how many Vendors offer it, which is the count of its Visible Offers, since a Vendor has one Offer for a Part at most; whether any is in stock; and its Lowest Price, the Shown Price of the first in-stock Visible Offer in `inTheOrderOfAPartsOffers`, so that it is marked converted exactly when that Offer's Shown Price is, and of equal prices it is the one a Part page lists first. `offered` describes a Part and never decides whether it is listed.
- **One statement, one moment.** Every public read that answers a price or the rate takes the current Platform Rate once, as a subquery (`currentPlatformRate`), in the statement that reads its rows, its order, its page and its total, so that one answer never mixes two rates. A list's page and total come from `onePageOf` (`infrastructure/database/paging.ts`), which both paged lists share, so that a page past the end still answers its total. The Query reads now once from the API's clock. Drizzle names a subquery's field by its alias alone, so each alias in these statements is a name no table in the statement has as a column; the Stores say so for whoever adds one.
- **The orders are written once**, in `infrastructure/offer/visible-offers-order.ts`: OFF-11's order (in stock first, then the lowest Shown Price as answered, those with none after the others, then the most recent Confirmation); `inTheOrderOfAPartsOffers`, OFF-11's then the Vendor's name and the Offer's identifier; and `inTheOrderOfAVendorsOffers`, the catalog's order of the eight Categories first (`placeOfCategory`, `infrastructure/part/category-order.ts`), then OFF-11's, the Part's name and the Offer's identifier. A Category's list orders its Parts `lowest_amount asc nulls last, any_in_stock desc, part.name asc, part.id asc`: a Lowest Price first, the lowest first, then in-stock Parts with none, then the Parts every Vendor has out of stock (BRW-1).
- **The Stores**, split by read in the code review: `VisiblePartStore` reads a Category's Visible Parts, their filters and one Visible Part, and `PlatformFiguresStore` the platform's figures, each a port in `domain/offer`, implemented by `DatabaseVisiblePartStore` and `DatabasePlatformFiguresStore`; a Part's and a Vendor's Visible Offers stay in the Offer Store. The Queries are `ListVisiblePartsQuery`, `ReadVisiblePartsFiltersQuery`, `ReadVisiblePartQuery`, `ListVisibleOffersOfPartQuery`, `ListVisibleOffersOfVendorQuery` and `ReadPlatformFiguresQuery` in `domain/offer`, and `ReadVisibleVendorQuery` in `domain/vendor`, which reads the Vendor through `VendorStore.readById` and `Vendor.isActive()`.

_The filters' statement, in the domain._

- `domain/specification/filters.ts`, beside the specification lists, states each Category's filters once, each a specification BRW-2 names, at its place in the Part's document, with a parameter of its own in camel case: the CPU's `socket` and `cores` (`cores.total`); the CPU cooler's `waterCooled` (`water_cooled`, "Air or liquid") and `radiatorSize`; the motherboard's `socket`, `formFactor` and `ramType` (`memory.ram_type`); RAM's `ramType`, `capacity` and `speed`; storage's `driveType` (`storage_type`, "SSD or HDD"), `capacity` and `interface`; the GPU's `chipset` and `memory`; the case's `formFactor`; and the PSU's `wattage` and `efficiencyRating`. A parameter two Categories share asks the same kind of value of both, and none is a parameter the list has already (`size` is paging's, so the radiator's is `radiatorSize`).
- `Filter.of` finds each specification at its path in its Category's list, so a filter's kind, unit and "0 means not known" come from the list. It answers none for a path the list lacks or a specification that holds a list, and the statement keeps only the filters it answers, so no domain code throws (ADR-0004); a unit test of the API, `test/domain/specification/filters.spec.ts`, proves the table whole against BRW-2 (the code review's fixes).
- **The manufacturer** is every Category's filter, but a Part's own field and no specification, so it is the list's own `manufacturer` parameter, not an entry of the statement.
- **A filter's kind** is `number` (a whole number), `text` (a fixed set or free text) or `yesNo`. A value asked is read by its kind, in Western digits, `true` or `false`, or as the text, and then checked by its specification's own `check`. Several values of one filter match a Part holding any of them, different filters must all match, and a Part missing a filter's specification matches no value of it. The Store compares JSON with JSON, `specificationAt(…) in (…)`, so that a number matches a number and text matches text; `specificationAt` writes the path into the statement, each name an SQL string with its quotes doubled, since PostgreSQL's `jsonb_agg(distinct x order by x)` wants the same expression twice.
- **Its values** are the distinct values the Category's Visible Parts hold, before any choice, so the choices never shift under the Shopper: numbers by size, text alphabetically, no counts, a filter with none left out.
- **The request declares each filter's parameter from the statement**: `ListVisiblePartsRequest` applies the decorators to each parameter `filterParameters()` gives, so no other file names them, the pipe's whitelist takes them, and the description lists each with the Categories and paths it filters, in words; the client types them (`socket?: Array<string>`, `cores?: Array<number>`, `waterCooled?: Array<boolean>`). A parameter given once is read as a list of one.
- **A new filter** is a change to the statement and then `pnpm api-client:write`. The Shopper app names each in its language, `SearchParts.filterNames`, and a type check in `value-words.tsx` fails `pnpm check` until a new one is named; the stand-in API shows it once added to its own list in `stand-in-api.ts`.

| Address | What it is |
|---|---|
| `/{language}` | The home page: the eight Categories, each with its count of Visible Parts and its first Part's Lowest Price, one Part on its plate, and the platform's figures |
| `/{language}/categories/{category}` | A Category page, with `search`, `manufacturer`, each of its filters, `lowest`, `highest` and `page` in its query |
| `/{language}/parts/{partId}` | A Part page |
| `/{language}/vendors/{vendorId}` | A Vendor page, with `page` in its query and `#offers` when it leads to another page of Offers |

| `/photos/{photoId}` | A Part's photo, the one address with no language |

- **A Category in the address** is its key in lower case with hyphens (`cpu`, `cpu-cooler`, `motherboard`, `ram`, `storage`, `gpu`, `case`, `psu`), mapped once in the `category` entity, `namesInTheAddress`, with `categoryAddress` for a link and `categoryOfTheAddress` for the route. An unknown Category, `CPU` among them, answers the not-found page with 404.
- **A Category page's query** is read and written by two plain functions of the `search-parts` feature, `readCategoryQuery(address, offered)` and `categoryPageAddress`, with `readPriceRange`, `choicesOn`, `nothingOn` and `addressOfTheForm`, against the API's answer of the Category's filters. The search's words are read with Arabic-Indic and Eastern Arabic-Indic digits as Western ones. A page counts from 1 in the address, and from 0 at the API; one that cannot be read is the first, and one past 1,000,001 the last the API pages to. A parameter or a value the filters do not offer, and a price range in a Category none of whose Visible Parts has a Lowest Price, are dropped and never sent; a repeated parameter of a filter is read once a value, in the order the filters offer them. Each end of a price range is read through `westernNumberIn`, so Arabic-Indic digits and the Arabic separators are read, and must be a whole number of at most 15 digits; one that is not, one too large, and a lowest above the highest are not sent, and the form says why under the field. Without the filters' answer only the search and the page are read, and the list is not asked for.
- **An address holds only what is on**, in the form's order: the search, the manufacturers, each filter as the API orders them, the price range, the page. Links the page writes, a chip that is on, Clear all and the pages, are written from the query as read, so they drop what was dropped. _What a page reads, and what it answers._

- **Every page is rendered on the server for each request**: it waits for Next's `connection()`, so it is never built ahead, then asks the API through `@pcp/api-client`, its reads at once, in the chosen currency from `chosenCurrency()`, each with `cache: 'no-store'`; React's `cache` gives a page and its preview one reading a request. **The compatibility module's conventions**

No other decision of the module has an ADR, as the owner decided on 8 November 2026, since undoing any of them loses no data: the sets, the rules and the needed specifications are code, and the GPUs' zero sockets and the empty lists of M.2 slots come back from OpenDB at the pinned commit. The owner's decisions are in [its spec](../compatibility/spec.md), its tickets and [its code review's fixes](../compatibility/review-fixes.md); the tickets record what was found and why.

_The library's public face._

- `@pcp/compatibility` is plain TypeScript, with no Effect, no npm package and no other project, its test files included, as its `scope:shared` tags enforce, and its outcomes are plain values, not success and failure (ADR-0004). It is imported through its single entry file, `src/index.ts`, and runs the same in the API, on the Shopper app's server and in the browser.
- **`judgeBuild(build)`** answers the outcome of every rule that applies to the Build, in rule order, each a `RuleOutcome`: `{ rule, outcome, parts }`, the outcome `passes`, `problem` or `warning`, or `{ rule, outcome: 'unchecked', missing }`, each missing value a `MissingSpecification`, `{ part, path }`. A Problem or a Warning names the Parts whose values make it, every counted kit or drive among them for a rule that counts; a pass names every Part the rule judged; an unchecked rule names each missing value that could change its outcome, once. Parts come in the catalog's order of Categories, and kits and drives in the Build's order, each by the identifier the caller gave it.
- **`isCompatible(category, part, build)`** (CMP-4) answers whether a Part, typed by its Category through `PartOf<C>`, is compatible with the Build. It puts the Part in place of the Build's Part of its Category for the six Categories a Build holds one of, and beside the Build's kits or drives with a quantity of one, whatever quantity it carries, and answers false only when judging that Build finds a Compatibility Problem naming the Part's identifier. It judges no rule itself, so the picker and the Build page give one answer. A Compatibility Warning, an unchecked rule and a Problem the Build already had that does not name the Part leave it compatible. A kit or a drive the Build already holds is counted once more, as adding one more of it would be. A rule that counts names every Part it counts by a known value, so while a Build has such a Problem, more modules than slots or a PSU too weak for the CPU alone, every kit whose modules are known, or every GPU whose TDP is known, is not compatible, since it would add to the Problem, and one whose value is missing stays compatible.
- **`estimatedPowerDrawOf(build)`** (CMP-5) answers `{ known: true, watts }` for a Build with a CPU, its TDP plus the GPU's, 0 W without a GPU, plus 100 W; `{ known: false, missing }`, naming each missing TDP; and `undefined` for a Build without a CPU. Rule 12 compares the same draw, `drawOf` in `estimated-power-draw.ts`, so a TDP is added up in one place, and no Shopper code adds one.
- **`neededSpecificationsOf(category)`** answers a Category's needed specifications by their paths, in the order the rules first read them (see _The needed specifications and the fixed sets_, below).
- Beside them are the types the library takes and gives: `Build`, `Part`, `PartAndQuantity`, `CategoryKey`, each Category's specifications (`CpuSpecifications` to `PsuSpecifications`, with `PcieSlots` and `M2Slot`), `RuleOutcome`, `MissingSpecification`, `EstimatedPowerDraw` and `PartOf`; and the fixed sets, each with its values as a type.

_The Build it reads, and what its caller leaves out._

- **A Build** holds at most one each of CPU, CPU cooler, motherboard, GPU, case and PSU, and any number of RAM kits and drives, each with its quantity, at least one (BLD-1), under the API's Category keys, `cpu`, `cpuCooler`, `motherboard`, `ram`, `storage`, `gpu`, `case` and `psu`, which the library names `CategoryKey`. An unfinished Build lacks some, and an empty one holds none. **A Part** is `{ id, specifications }`: its identifier is the caller's, its Part's identifier, by which every outcome names it; its specifications are its needed specifications, in OpenDB's names and nesting as the catalog holds them (ADR-0005), typed for each Category in `build.ts`, where `SpecificationsOf` reads a Category's, and each value of a fixed set typed by its set.
- **The contract.** Whoever hands the rules a Part, `builder` first, leaves out every specification the catalog counts as missing, through the specification list's own rule, `Specification.isMissingIn(value)` (see **The offers module's conventions**): nothing, null, an empty text, an empty choice of several or list of text, and a 0 where 0 means "not known". A field of a row, such as a PCIe slot's lanes, may be left out the same way. The library holds no copy of that rule: a value it is given is known, and a value it is not given is missing. It does not check that a quantity is at least one. The API's function that hands a stored Part to the rules, and any endpoint that judges a Build or a Part, are `builder`'s.

_When a rule applies, and when it is unchecked._

- **A rule applies** to a Build that holds a Part of each Category it involves (CMP-2), and an empty Build gets no outcome. A Category a rule reads without needing it in order to apply, such as rule 12's GPU, is read when the Build holds a Part of it. Rules 13 to 16 involve the drives, so they apply to a Build with any drive beside their motherboard, case or PSU, and pass one with no drive of their kind whatever that Part holds, as the owner kept for rules 13, 15 and 16 on 8 November 2026.
- **One outcome per rule**: a Compatibility Problem when the values present prove one; otherwise unchecked when a missing value could still change the outcome, to a Problem or to a Warning; otherwise a Warning or a pass, as the values say. A missing value is taken to be any value its specification could hold: any value of its fixed set, yes or no, or any number, 0 among them, though the catalog stores no 0 where 0 means "not known". So a rule is unchecked only when a missing specification could change its outcome: an ordinary motherboard passes rule 21 whatever the case says, a Build with no SATA drive passes rule 13 whatever the board's SATA ports, and a cooler that fits passes rule 17 whether or not it says it is liquid cooled. An unchecked rule is never passed, and a Problem the known values prove is a Problem though another value is missing.
- **A Part whose kind a rule cannot tell**, such as a drive with no interface for rule 13 or a cooler with no "Liquid cooled" for rule 17, is one the rule may involve, and the same principle decides; such a Part is never among those a Problem names.
- **Only rules 11, 15 and 20 can give a Compatibility Warning** (CMP-3). A judgment is `problemWhen(problem, { warningWhen })`, the Problem first, so a Warning's finding need not leave the Problem out.

_Each rule stated once._

- Each rule is in `src/rules/rule-NN.ts`, listed in rule order in `rules.ts`, and stated with `rule({ number, involves, readsWhenHeld, judge })` from `statement.ts`. `involves` gives the paths the rule reads in each Category it involves and `readsWhenHeld` those of a Category it reads when held, both a `PathsRead`, by Category. Its judgment reads each value through `read(path)`, typed from the statement, so a path it does not state, or a misspelt one, fails the type check, and a value of a fixed set comes typed by its set. A rule that reads a list of rows, such as a motherboard's PCIe slots, names the list. From the statements the library answers each Category's needed specifications.
- **The findings**, in `rules/findings.ts`: a condition holds, does not, or may, naming the missing values that could decide it. `whatever` takes a missing value to be each value of its fixed set, or of `yesOrNo`; `some`, `not`, `and` and `or` join findings; `equals`, `includes` and `someRow` read a value, a list and a list's rows; `exceeds` compares two amounts; and `theBuildHolds` puts in a Problem a Part whose values a rule does not read, as rule 8's GPU.
- **The amounts**, in `rules/amounts.ts`: a count runs from a least to a most, with no most when a count it holds is missing. `product`, `total`, `counted` (a kit or a drive as many times as the Build holds it), `nothing`, `amountWhatever` (a count whatever a missing value is), `together` (a limit that several values of one Part make, such as a board's SATA ports of both speeds), `totalOfRows`, `larger` (the larger of several, such as a GPU's two kinds of 12-pin socket) and `times`.
- **A value read** is a `Reading`, its Part, its path and its value or none, and `rowsOf` reads each row of a list at one field, named as the API names it, `pcie_slots.1.lanes` (`rules/reading.ts`). **The drives' readings** that several rules share are in `rules/drives.ts`: `isM2`, `sataDrives`, `m2Drives` and `m2DrivesNotSata`, written with `drivesWhose`, the drives whose value at a path is of a kind, and `driveWhose`, one drive.
- Each dependency runs one way: rule 12 reads the estimated power draw, the draw and the findings read the amounts, and none of them reads back. Naming each missing value once, `withoutRepeats`, is beside its type in `outcome.ts`.

_Each rule's reading of OpenDB's values._

- [The compatibility spec](../compatibility/spec.md)'s table gives what each rule needs in a Build to apply and how it reads OpenDB's values, with the imported Parts each reading concerns, and the rule notes under **Compatibility Rules** the readings the owner decided. In short: rule 1's "2 x \<socket\>" boards take a CPU of their socket, its "Integrated …" boards make any CPU a Problem, and its Narrow boards take a CPU of LGA 2011-3; rule 7's Thin Mini-ITX and Flex ATX boards fit where Mini-ITX and Micro ATX do; rule 8's empty list of PCIe slots is a board with none, and a row with no lanes is unknown; rule 10 compares a fraction of a slot as it is; rule 11 counts a GPU's 6-pin and 8-pin sockets together against the PSU's 6+2-pin connectors, and needs as many of its 12VHPWR cables as the larger of the GPU's 12VHPWR and 12V-2x6 counts (the owner's decision of 8 November 2026); rule 12 compares ten times the PSU's wattage with thirteen times the draw, so a PSU of exactly 1.3 times passes; rules 13 and 16 count a drive as a SATA drive by its interface, SATA 1.5, 3.0 or 6.0 Gb/s, and its form factor, which is no M.2 size, since a drive of an M.2 size and such an interface is an M.2 SATA drive (the owner's decision of 8 November 2026); rule 14 lets a slot keyed M take any M.2 drive, one keyed B only an M.2 SATA drive and one keyed E none, and leaves a slot's sizes alone; rule 15 reads a drive by its form factor, lets a 2.5-inch drive take a free 3.5-inch bay, and warns of a 2.5-inch drive in a case with no internal bay; rule 17 judges only an air cooler; rule 18 lets no form factor stand in for another; and rule 20 counts the board's CPU power connectors' entries against the PSU's EPS 8-pin connectors, its Problem, a PSU with none, naming the PSU alone.
- Rules 5, 6 and 13 to 16 count every kit and drive times its quantity, and rules 1, 2, 3, 4, 7 and 18 compare whole values of the fixed sets, so two spellings of one value cannot make a false Problem.
- **Two Problems cannot arise from the catalog's data today**: the catalog reads a PSU's 0 EPS connectors and 0 PCIe 6+2-pin connectors as not known, so rule 20's "no EPS connector at all" and rule 11's "no such cable and no 6+2-pin connector" come out unchecked. The library judges them as the rules say when it is given a 0, and its tests prove both.

_The needed specifications and the fixed sets, read from the library._

- **A specification is needed** when the rules' statements name its path for its Category. The API's `SpecificationList` takes its Category's key, the library's `CategoryKey`, before its specifications, and keeps the paths `neededSpecificationsOf` answers: `getNeeded()` gives its specifications at those paths, in the list's order, and `isNeeded` answers for one of them or for a field of a row, which is never needed, since a rule that reads a list of rows names the list. `Specification` has no `needed` option, so no list can mark a needed specification by hand. The Categories endpoint, a Part's missing needed specifications and Missing specifications read them so, and cannot drift from the rules.
- **They are what the lists marked by hand before**, with one addition: a CPU cooler's `water_cooled`, "Liquid cooled", which rule 17 reads to tell an air cooler from a liquid one, so a cooler's form, its page and Missing specifications mark it as they mark the others; one imported cooler lacks it. A path the statements name that its list lacks marks nothing, and the API still starts (ADR-0004); the Categories endpoint's test, whose table is written out from the MVP spec's and not read from the platform, proves that none does, and that no field of a row is needed.
- **The fixed sets** are in the library's `src/fixed-sets.ts`, which the API's lists import: `sockets`, `ramTypes`, `motherboardFormFactors`, `powerSupplyFormFactors`, `storageFormFactors`, `storageInterfaces` and `m2SlotKeys`, each with its values as a type, such as `Socket`, so that a rule naming a misspelt value fails the type check; and `gpuPowerSockets`, with `GpuPowerSocket`, the four kinds of a GPU's power socket, which rule 11 counts, the GPU's list makes its entries from and the import reads. They moved unchanged from the API's domain, with the comments that say why a value is in a set or left out. A new value, such as the socket of a CPU released later, is added there, once, for the Admin's forms and the rules alike.
- The Shopper app's list of the libraries it does not use yet, in its Shared layer's `config` segment, proves that the library resolves through `sockets`, until `builder` imports it for real.

_The import's reading of a GPU's power sockets._

- **A GPU whose TDP is above 75 W**, the most a PCIe slot supplies, **and which holds 0 for each of its four power sockets**, 6-pin, 8-pin, 12VHPWR and 12V-2x6, is imported without the four, as not known, as the owner decided on 7 November 2026 (`compatibility` ticket 07): read as true, the zeros would let rule 11 pass such a GPU with any PSU. `withoutPowerSocketsNotKnown`, in `opendb-record.ts`, reads the document the GPU list's check keeps, and counts the four among the values left out with the reason `noPowerSocketAbove75W`, which the report prints as "0 for each power socket, where the GPU draws more than the 75 W a PCIe slot supplies", one line for each socket's path. At the pinned commit that is 695 GPUs, such as the Asus ROG STRIX GeForce RTX 3080 Ti at 350 W. A GPU of 75 W or less with no socket keeps its zeros, since a slot powers it (299 GPUs), and so does one whose TDP is missing (4); a GPU with any socket keeps all four counts as given.
- Rule 11 is then unchecked for such a GPU, and Missing specifications asks for its four sockets once a Vendor offers it. The reading is the import's alone: the Admin may still give any GPU any counts, and the GPU's list still reads a 0 socket as true, as its comment says. The development database was brought to it in place on 7 November 2026, after the owner's yes, by a guarded block tried first on a throwaway copy, and an empty list of M.2 slots on a board of a socket of 2017 or later is read the same way for rule 14 (see _The import_, under **The catalog's conventions**).

**Worth knowing about the toolchain**

- _One copy of NestJS._ pnpm keeps a copy of a package for each set of peers it resolves. `core` and the API once got a copy of NestJS each, and NestJS cannot inject a class from one copy into the other: the API's tests failed with "Nest can't resolve dependencies". The root manifest therefore lists `supports-color`, which nothing imports, and `pnpm-workspace.yaml` says why. If that failure returns, check that `apps/api` and `libs/core` link to the same `@nestjs/core`.
- _Packages published as ES modules only._ Jest loads CommonJS and leaves `node_modules` untransformed. The shared Jest preset names the packages that SWC has to transform for it. `jose` was the first; `@keycloak/keycloak-admin-client` and the packages it needs, Microsoft's six Kiota packages, `camelize-ts` and `url-template`, followed. The built API loads the admin client through Node 24's `require` of an ES module.
- _Install scripts._ pnpm stops an install when a dependency has an install script that `pnpm-workspace.yaml` does not list. A script is read before its package is listed, and one that reports to a third party is listed as not to be run.
- _A serve that is killed from outside_ can leave Nx's own processes behind, and the next serve then waits for them. Their command lines contain `nx.js serve` and `run-executor.js`.
- _Old results that pass for new._ Nx keeps one local cache under the home directory, which every checkout and worktree on the machine shares, and its cached type check once let a type error through (`browse` ticket 02); `tsc --build` also skips a project whose `*.tsbuildinfo` is newer than files put back with their old times (ticket 04). So a commit's gate deletes every `*.tsbuildinfo` outside `node_modules` and runs `pnpm check --skip-nx-cache`.
- _Node's fetch on a reset connection_ can throw, uncaught; the design skills guide says what it means for the two inspectors (`browse`'s code review).
- _What Nx 23.3 prints._ Since 23.3 Nx keeps a task's output to itself unless it is asked for static output, so a script that reads or shows what a task printed asks for `--output-style=static`, as `check-integration.mjs`, `check-platform.mjs` and `check-boundaries.mjs` do. Its oxlint executor runs Oxlint once from the repository's root, prints its JSON report again over several lines, and names each file from the root (follow-up ticket 08's code review). In an agent's environment, which Nx tells by variables such as `AI_AGENT` and `CLAUDECODE`, an output style left unnamed becomes `summary`, so even one task prints only Nx's summary, while a person sees what one task prints, in a terminal and in a file alike. `compatibility` ticket 08 had to run the import again without those variables to read its report, so since 8 November 2026 `pnpm db:migrate` and `pnpm catalog:import` ask for `--output-style=static` themselves. An agent reads the import's report and the migrations' outcome, which `pnpm services:up` prints through `pnpm db:migrate`, as a person does, and a person sees the two commands' output printed plainly, not in Nx's terminal UI. Another script that runs a task an agent must read asks for it too.

## Boundaries

**Always**

- Use the terms in CONTEXT.md, exactly as written, in code, tests and interface text. If a needed concept has no term, add it to CONTEXT.md first.
- Build every Shopper and Vendor screen in all three languages and both directions from its first version.
- Design for a phone-width screen first.
- Record every price and in-stock change (OFF-10).
- Check a library's current documentation before using it.

**Ask the owner first**

- Adding, removing or changing a Compatibility Rule, the power formula, the 14-day expiry or the rounding of Converted Prices.
- Building anything listed under [Not in the MVP](#not-in-the-mvp).
- Adding any service that costs money or sends messages.
- Changing how Parts or Offers are stored once real Vendor data exists.

**Never**

- Promote, highlight or reorder an Offer for payment.
- Fetch the Platform Rate from an exchange-rate service.
- Let a Vendor create or edit a Part.
- Show a Converted Price without the "≈" mark.
- Show Shoppers an Expired Offer or an Offer from a deactivated Vendor.
- Present an unchecked Compatibility Rule as passed.
- Show a Vendor's Referral count to another Vendor or to Shoppers.
- Commit passwords, keys or other secrets.

## Success criteria

The MVP is finished when all of these hold:

1. On a fresh installation, one command imports the catalog: about 26,000 Parts across the eight Categories, none of them visible to Shoppers.
2. The Admin creates a Vendor and sends its login details through WhatsApp; the Vendor logs in with the temporary password and is made to replace it.
3. A Vendor adds an Offer quoted only in USD. A Shopper sees the Part with that USD price, and in IQD sees a price marked "≈", converted at the Platform Rate and rounded to the nearest 250 IQD.
4. An Offer left unconfirmed for more than 14 days disappears from every Shopper page and returns when the Vendor confirms it.
5. A Shopper on a phone assembles a Complete Build in each of the three languages. Incompatible Parts are hidden from the picker; with the switch on, picking one makes the Build page list the Compatibility Problem.
6. The Build page shows the cheapest total across Vendors and each Vendor's own total with its missing Parts.
7. A shared Build link opens the same Parts with current prices. Copying it and changing the copy leaves the shared Build unchanged.
8. Tapping "send on WhatsApp" opens a chat with the Vendor containing the Build's link, and that Vendor's Referral count rises by one, visible to the Admin and to that Vendor only.
9. A Vendor downloads its sheet, changes some prices, adds one row with a known part number and one with an unknown one, and uploads it. The prices change, one Offer is created, and the unknown row is reported and can be turned into a Part Request.
10. The Admin fulfils that Part Request and the Vendor's Offer goes live.
11. Every Compatibility Rule has passing tests for each outcome it can have.
12. A link to a Part page or a shared Build, pasted into WhatsApp, shows a preview.
13. Every Shopper page and Vendor portal screen is usable at 360 pixels wide in English and in Arabic.
