# 04: Drives, clearances and the PSU's connectors: rules 13 to 21

**Spec:** [the compatibility spec](../spec.md)

**What to build:** The library judges the last nine rules: the drives against the motherboard's ports and slots, the case's bays and the PSU's SATA power connectors (rules 13 to 16); an air cooler's height and the PSU's form factor and length against the case (rules 17 to 19); the PSU's CPU power connectors against the board's (rule 20); and a back-connect board against the case (rule 21). Rules 15 and 20 can give a Compatibility Warning. With this ticket every one of the 21 rules is stated. The tests are the proof, written before the code.

**Blocked by:** 02

**Status:** resolved

- [x] **Rules 13 to 21**, as the spec's table reads OpenDB's values:
  - a SATA drive, for rules 13 and 16, is one whose interface is SATA 1.5, 3.0 or 6.0 Gb/s, and an M.2 SATA, mSATA, SAS or U.2 drive is not; the board's ports are its SATA 6 Gb/s and 3 Gb/s ports together; an M.2 drive needs no SATA power;
  - an M.2 drive, for rule 14, is one whose form factor is an M.2 size; a slot keyed M takes any M.2 drive, a slot keyed B only an M.2 SATA drive, a slot keyed E none, and a slot with no key is unknown; no slot's sizes are compared;
  - rule 15 reads a drive's form factor whatever its interface, lets a 2.5-inch drive take a free 3.5-inch bay, and gives a Warning for a 2.5-inch drive in a case that gives no internal bay at all, as the rule notes say;
  - rule 17 judges an air cooler, one whose "Liquid cooled" is no, against the case's tallest cooler, and a liquid cooler passes;
  - rule 18 lets no form factor stand in for another;
  - rule 20 counts the board's CPU power sockets as the entries of its CPU power connectors, against the PSU's EPS 8-pin connectors, as the rule notes say;
  - rules 13 to 16 count every drive times its quantity.
- [x] The library's tests are written before the code, and they prove each of these:
  - for each of rules 13 to 21, every outcome it can have, and for rules 15 and 20 their Compatibility Warnings;
  - two of one drive count as two drives in rules 13 to 16, and as many drives as the ports, slots, bays or connectors pass while one more does not;
  - an M.2 SATA drive counts against an M.2 slot and not a SATA port, takes a B-keyed slot, and needs no SATA power; an NVMe drive takes no B-keyed slot; no drive takes an E-keyed slot;
  - rule 15's 2.5-inch drive in a free 3.5-inch bay passes, a 2.5-inch drive with every bay taken is a Problem, and a case with no internal bay is a Warning for a 2.5-inch drive and a Problem for a 3.5-inch one;
  - rule 20's board with two CPU power sockets passes with two EPS connectors, gives a Warning with one, and finds a Problem with none, a 0 given to the library directly, since the catalog stores no 0 there;
  - rule 18's SFX supply in a case that lists only ATX is a Problem;
  - a cooler or a PSU exactly at the case's limit fits;
  - unchecked only when it matters: a Build with no SATA drive passes rule 13 whatever the board's SATA ports; an ordinary board passes rule 21 whatever the case says, and a case that takes back-connect boards passes it whatever the board says; a cooler that fits under the case's limit passes rule 17 whether or not it says it is liquid cooled; a drive with no interface leaves rule 13 unchecked when it could tip the count, and judged when it could not; a slot with no key leaves rule 14 unchecked only when the drives could need it.

**How the rules count drives.** Ticket 02 left counting a drive whose kind a missing value hides. `amountWhatever(reading, values, count)`, in `src/rules/findings.ts`, is the amount form of `whatever`: a value the Part holds decides the count, and a missing one is taken to be each value of its fixed set, so the count runs from the least of theirs to the most, naming the value unless they all count the same. It is about the Parts that each of them counts, so a drive whose kind is missing is never among the Parts a Problem names. `counted(part)` counts a kit or a drive as many times as the Build holds it, about that Part, and `nothing` counts a Part of another kind. `together(...values)` is a limit that values of one Part make together, such as a motherboard's SATA ports of both speeds or a case's bays of both sizes, about that Part, a missing value leaving it no most. `yesOrNo` is what a yes/no specification could hold, for `whatever` to take a missing one to be either. `src/rules/drives.ts` holds the readings that several rules share: `sataDrives`, the SATA drives of rules 13 and 16, and `isM2`, a drive of an M.2 size.

**Rules 13 to 21, as the spec's table reads them.**

- Rule 13 counts the SATA drives, those whose interface is SATA 1.5, 3.0 or 6.0 Gb/s, against the board's SATA 6 Gb/s and 3 Gb/s ports together. It applies to a Build with a motherboard and any drive, so a Build with no SATA drive passes it whatever the board's ports, as the spec's paragraph on unchecked rules has it. A drive whose interface is missing leaves it unchecked only when it could tip the count.
- Rule 14 reads a drive's form factor, to tell an M.2 drive, and its interface, since a slot keyed B takes only a drive whose interface is M.2 SATA. It finds a Problem when the M.2 drives that are not SATA outnumber the slots keyed M, or all the M.2 drives the slots keyed M or B; a slot keyed E counts for none. A slot with no key may have any key, and is named by its place among the slots, `m2_slots.1.key`, as the API names it. A missing list of slots may hold any number, and an empty one is a board with none. 3 imported drives give their interface as "SATA 6.0 Gb/s" and their form factor as M.2-2280: rules 13 and 16 count them as SATA drives by their interface, and rule 14 as M.2 drives that a slot keyed B does not take, as the table's readings say; only 2 imported slots are keyed B.
- Rule 15 reads a drive by its form factor, whatever its interface. It finds a Problem when the 3.5-inch drives outnumber the 3.5-inch bays, or the drives of both sizes the bays of both, a 2.5-inch drive taking a free 3.5-inch bay. When the case gives no internal bay at all, a 2.5-inch drive is a Warning and a 3.5-inch one a Problem, which names the 3.5-inch drives and the case, not a 2.5-inch drive beside them. An M.2, mSATA or PCIe drive needs no bay.
- Rule 16 counts the SATA drives as rule 13 does, through `sataDrives`, against the PSU's SATA power connectors, so an M.2 drive needs none. A PSU with no SATA power connector, a 0 the catalog stores as missing, is a Problem when the library is given it.
- Rule 17 judges an air cooler, one whose "Liquid cooled" is no, against the case's tallest cooler, equal fitting, and passes a liquid cooler. A cooler that does not say whether it is liquid cooled is taken to be either: one that fits passes, and one too tall is unchecked, naming `water_cooled`.
- Rule 18 finds a Problem when the case does not list the PSU's form factor, none standing in for another: an SFX, SFX-L, ATX/EPS or Flex ATX supply in a case that does not list it.
- Rule 19 compares the PSU's length with the case's longest PSU, equal fitting.
- Rule 20 counts the board's CPU power sockets as the entries of its CPU power connectors, against the PSU's EPS 8-pin connectors, as `problemWhen(not(exceeds(connectors, 0)), { warningWhen: exceeds(sockets, connectors) })`, the form ticket 03 proposed. A PSU with no EPS connector at all is a Problem whatever the board's sockets, which the PSU alone makes, so it names the PSU and not the board. One with at least one but fewer than the board has sockets is a Warning, naming both.
- Rule 21 reads the board's answer first: an ordinary board passes whatever the case says, a back-connect board is judged by the case's answer, and a case that takes such boards passes whatever the board says.
- Rules 13 to 16 count every drive times its quantity. A Problem names the Parts whose values make it, the drives it counts among them; a drive of another kind, and one whose kind is missing, is not named.

**A note for the owner: empty lists of M.2 slots.** The catalog counts an empty list of rows as a value, not as missing, so a board whose list of M.2 slots is empty reaches the library as a board with none, as the spec reads rule 8's empty list of PCIe slots, and rule 14 finds a Problem in any M.2 drive on it. 723 of the 3,691 imported motherboards hold an empty list (read from the development database on 8 November 2026, imported Parts only). 687 of them are of sockets older than 2017, such as LGA 1150, LGA 1155 and AM3+, where a board without an M.2 slot is usual; their most common chipsets are Intel's H110 (40), Z77 (38), H81 (36), H61 (35) and B85 (31). 36 are of sockets of 2017 or later, such as AM4 and LGA 1200, among them the ASRock X570 Taichi and two ASRock B860 Challenger WiFi boards, which have M.2 slots that OpenDB does not list. An empty list is not missing, so Missing specifications does not ask for them. Nothing changes here: whether the catalog should read an empty list of M.2 slots as missing, as it reads a 0 for SATA 6 Gb/s ports, is the owner's to decide, and the question goes to the owner with the module's code review.
